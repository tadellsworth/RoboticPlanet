Terrafall · Field Manual

Land. Build. Expand.

Your robots have touched down on a dusty alien world. This manual covers everything you need to run the colony: what each building does, how goods flow, how batteries and terrain shape your plans, and how to beat the rival.

The goal

Gather raw resources, turn them into better goods, build more robots and spread across the planet. Pick a mode before each game.

Versus

A rival colony lands at the far end of the map. Capture its Command Base to win. If it captures yours, you lose. A game runs about 20–40 minutes, longer on hilly maps. Pick another way to win with Victory on the new-game screen (see Ways to win), and a rival that has clearly lost surrenders.

Missions

The tutorial campaign: hand-picked planets in seven chapters, each mission with its own goals and Easy, Normal and Hard levels, from your first building to all-out war. See Missions.

Colony

No rival and no clock. Build supply chains and spread out. Milestones pop up at 10, 25, 40, 60 and 80% of the land your bots can reach. Free play has every building and missiles included (Nuclear race adds uranium and nuclear silos), to experiment with, plus the Research Lab, and the Murmur to fight off: see The Murmur & research.

New-game settings

Second biomeOn Immense or Planetary, select a map type and a surface biome to add a second region. Ground and vegetation blend along a seeded frontier. The primary biome covers about 65% of the land and sets weather and world rules. The Hollow and Argos keep their own scenery.
Map sizeStandard: Small 30×42, Medium 40×56, Large 50×70, Huge 60×84, Immense 84×118 or Planetary 120×168 tiles. Planetary supports up to two colonies. Its other shapes keep their proportions within a 20,160-tile budget. Rivals allow more time for long marches at the two largest sizes. The menu remembers the size separately for each mode.
Map typeStandard. Long haul: a narrow planet three quarters as wide and 1.7 times as long, with the bases far apart, built for long supply lines. Chokepoint: a ragged belt of crags across the middle with one to three narrow channels winding through it, different every game; whoever holds the channels holds the map. Islands: each base on its own island, with two or three medium islands in the middle and a couple of islets, all a wide sea apart, so the way across is a long, expensive bridge or a ship. Crater: a sunken crater in the middle holding the richest fields (and Uranium in a Nuclear race), with one natural ramp in from each side. Plateaus: each base on a high plateau ringed by cliffs, with only two slopes down: easy to defend, slow to break out of. Sky islands: no water at all, just a home mesa for each side and around a dozen smaller flat-topped mesas between them, a chain of them always linking the sides, standing over a canyon floor far below, with sheer drops and no slopes down; cross between clifftops with Viaducts (a Ropeway can reach the floor, but there's little down there). Rift: a chasm splits the planet, crossed only by a few natural rock arches; hold yours with turrets and Mortar Pits (a Sky bridge is the slow way to open another). Ring valley: a massif too steep to climb fills the middle, with a valley round it on each side: two long ways round (a long tunnel is the only shortcut). Oases: a barren, crag-strewn waste; everything worth having is round your base and in a few rich oases joined by narrow passes. Crossroads: a mesa in each corner, two levels above an open lowland whose four arms meet at a rich plaza in the middle. Three slopes lead down off every mesa, one toward each neighbouring corner and one toward the plaza, so the high ground is worth holding but no wall: attacks can come up any of them. Your base stands on one mesa; with two players the two mesas no one holds are rich, and it is also the map for up to four players, a colony on each mesa. Lakes: many small lakes, roads weaving between them; bridges save long detours. Rift, Ring valley, Oases and Lakes are for two players. On every map you can cut more ramps in Paths. Tidal flats: low islands joined by sandbars, dry at low tide and under the sea at high tide (with a warning half a minute before): bots walk across at low tide, and anything still out on the flats when the sea comes in is washed ashore. Nothing can be built on the flats, so trucks still need bridges or ships. River delta: a wide braided river splits the planet, with side channels off each flank. Bots can wade the shallow fords (slowly); haulers and trucks need a bridge. The islet midstream holds Oil and Crystal, and the banks are fertile for Bio Farms. Volcano: a volcano in the middle with lava rivers running down its flanks (bridge them, or go round where they end) and the richest deposits on its slopes. Every few minutes it rumbles, then erupts, raining lava bombs on its slopes for a few seconds: they dent buildings (never wreck them) and hurt bots caught out there. Fault line: a faint scar runs across the middle of the planet. About 15 minutes in (warnings at two minutes and at 30 seconds) an earthquake opens it into a chasm: whatever stands on it falls in, and only two rock arches are left to cross (a Sky bridge in Paths opens another way). Grand Canyon (two players): each base on a high rim above rocky shelves and fertile river basins. The Ore- and Crystal-rich floor alternates between wide bends and narrows, and the river splits around two islands. Each rim has two single-lane natural descents, a switchback and a side-canyon trail. Robots wade at the fords, and ships can sail the river. Canyon maze: a labyrinth of narrow canyons between the bases, with Ore, Crystal, Oil and more in its dead ends (and a little stalk, Ore, Crystal and Stone round each base to start on). Ambushes are easy, artillery shoots over the walls, and a Tunnel can cut through a wall, at a price. The Hollow: the Murmur's cavern lair under the Bloom, always night and with its own look: caverns joined by passages, chasms, black lakes and daylight only down the breach shafts (see The Hollow).

Made for three or four players only (marked “3 players only”, “3–4 players only” or “4 players only”: the Players choice can't go lower on them): The Wheel (three): rock spokes run out between the colonies from a raised hub in the middle, the only ground on the top level, with the richest fields and one ramp up from each side; a rim road of open ground runs round the outside where the spokes stop. Go the long way round the rim, or fight for the hub. Three Mesas (three): each colony stands on a tall mesa that fills most of its third, with terraces stepping down round its edge and one way down, toward a rich basin in the middle. Between neighbouring mesas runs a narrow canyon, close enough to span with a Viaduct. (The computer rivals do it too, from about ten minutes in: watch your clifftops, on Plateaus as well.) Pinwheel (three or four): four curved ridges sweep out from a rich plaza in the middle, a colony between each pair; your way into the plaza bends toward the next colony round, and gaps at the ridges' outer ends lead round to both neighbours. Four Isles (three or four): every colony on its own island, with a rich island in the middle (where the Trade Post lands) and islets between; build a Port and ships, or long bridges. In 2 vs 2 a sandbar joins allies' islands: robots wade across, trucks still need a bridge. Twin Valleys (four, made for 2 vs 2): a mountain spine splits the planet into a west and an east valley, two colonies in each, crossed only by two passes with rich fields at both ends. In a team game your ally shares your valley. Long Haul 2v2 (four, made for 2 vs 2): the Long haul planet made wider and standing upright (it fits a phone held upright), two colonies side by side at each end, all four starts the same (mirrored left to right and end to end). The richest fields are strung along the long middle, richer the further out, round a plaza at the very centre with a Trade Post; the other posts come in pairs, down the middle line between allies or out at its flanks, the same for both teams. Picking it turns Teams: 2 vs 2 on (you can turn it off for a free-for-all of four): a war of supply lines, Depots, Freight Trucks, Road Trains and rail pushed up the map.
Unlimited resourcesFor trying things out: your Command Base stays stocked with 500 of every good, and your construction sites, factories, chargers and garrisons get whatever they're waiting for, so nothing waits on hauling. Rivals play as usual.
ChallengeModifiers that make a game harder, mixed as you like (multiplayer too): Half Fuel (Refineries and energy crops make Fuel at half speed), Endless dust storm (from 5 minutes in a dust storm that never ends: bots at two-thirds speed, Missiles grounded), Rival head start (Versus: each computer colony lands with twice the goods and 8 more robots), Scarce Crystal (half the Crystal in every cluster and Command Base), Fragile robots (every robot takes half again the damage) and No turrets (no Pulse Turrets, Minigun Nests, Mortar Pits or Disruptor Towers for you; the computer still builds them). The planet card, the briefing and the pause menu list the ones on. A win with one on earns its challenge badge in Records.
TerrainFlat, Gentle, Rolling, Rugged or Mountainous. Rougher maps have more cliff levels and fewer passes between them.
ResourcesScarce to Abundant: how many resource fields there are, how big they are and how long each deposit lasts. Normal is lean: enough near home to get going, and then you'll need to spread out for more. Rich and Abundant make for a more relaxed game (Rich holds nearly half as much again). Whatever the setting, deposits get richer towards the middle of the map. The missions are all played on Rich.
BiomeA region of Ithaca I, with its kind of ground under its name: the Rust (red desert), Greenreach (forest), the Polar Caps (polar snow), Cinderlands (volcanic ashlands), Argos (the moon), Emberwood (autumn forest), the Long Grass (savanna), the Bloom (alien bloom), Steamwells (geothermal basin), the Shoals (naval maps) or Blackwater (swamp): the planet's ground, water, rock and plant life, its sounds and its weather. The Forest grows in stands of spruce and fir and of leafy trees; the ashlands' lakes are lava that glows at night; on the Moon the lakes are frozen; the alien bloom's plants glow after dark. Steamwells has turquoise and amber pools, layered limestone and geysers. Pick a naval map to unlock the Shoals' coconut palms, seagulls and reef shallows. Small waves drift across liquid water. Watch the open water on naval maps for leaping fish and dolphins and the occasional whale spout. On Volcano maps and in Cinderlands, lava has drifting highlights and steam along the banks. Scenery only (plants never get in the way, and a road or building clears the plants on its tile), with one exception: no Murmur live on Argos, the moon. The Hollow map has its own underground look whatever you pick here.
MegastructuresOn (the default) or Off. On adds the Lance of Corvane, the Shield Dome, Arrival City, the Orbital Lift and (with the Great Uplink win) the Great Uplink, late-game giants, one of each per colony: see Megastructures.
Great Uplink winOff (the default) or On, beside Victory. On adds a second way to win, alongside whichever Victory you picked (teams too): build the Great Uplink and hold its transmission for 8 minutes, from 25 minutes in. It needs Megastructures on; not in the Hollow. See Megastructures.
Nuclear raceOn (the default) or Off. On scatters a few glowing Uranium deposits and adds the Nuclear Reactor and Nuclear Silo: see Nuclear race.
PlayersYou vs 1; You vs 2: a free-for-all against two rivals, red and green, on a round planet where the three starts are the same (Standard, Islands, Crater, Plateaus and Sky islands, and The Wheel and Three Mesas, made for three), or on one of the four-quarter maps; You vs 3: against red, green and purple rivals on a four-quarter map (Crossroads, Pinwheel, Four Isles or Twin Valleys), one colony in each corner quarter, or on Long Haul 2v2, two colonies side by side at each end. (Versus only.)
TeamsWith You vs 3: Free-for-all (every colony for itself) or 2 vs 2, you and the red colony (VERMILION) against green and purple (VIRIDIAN and TYRIAN). Team games are won by Capture. Picking Long Haul 2v2 turns 2 vs 2 on.
RivalEasy, Medium, Normal or Hard (Versus only). Harder rivals produce faster, attack sooner with bigger armies and raid more often. Medium sits between Easy and Normal.
Rival doctrineHow the computer plays: the Steward (balanced; the default), Random (each rival gets one no other rival has), the Warden (turtle), Salvager (raider), Herald (rusher) or Architect (tycoon). Point at one, or tap it, and the line under them says in depth how it plays. With three or four players, Each rival sets one for each computer colony. See Rival doctrines.
The MurmurOff, Quiet or Active (the default), in Versus and Colony alike: the planet's native creatures, nobody's side. Their nests sit out in the wilds; every night their tunnels spread toward the colonies' power and packs come up through the exits to raid you all. Quiet has fewer nests, slower tunnels and smaller packs. With them on there's no Always day (they live by the night). Never on Argos, the moon, or on Sky islands or Four Isles (no room for their nests): Off and greyed there. See The Murmur & research.
Planetary eventsOff (the default) or On. Dust storms, Ringfall, solar flares and the Wren, a trader ship, every few minutes: see Planetary events.
PlanetWith the same settings, a planet number always makes the same map. Tap New planet for a different one. Save keeps the planet (with its map settings) in Saved planets below it, to play again any time; × forgets one. In a game, Save this planet in the pause menu does the same.

Your first ten minutes

Tap Build, pick a building and tap a highlighted tile. Each card shows how many of that building you already have. Check the preview, then tap Build here or tap the same tile again. Placing a site is free, but Haulers bring its materials by road: tap the new site and press Lay road (or draw one yourself in Paths) to join it to the ring of road round your Command Base; free workers pave it in a few seconds. You can have six sites going at once.

  1. Fuel StationFirst of all, beside the road ring round your Command Base. Robots run on batteries and the base can't charge them; the station runs on the 10 Fuel you land with.
  2. HarvesterNext to the teal stalk groves. The preview says how many groves are in reach; aim for plenty.
  3. QuarryBy the grey boulders. Its Stone becomes Cement, which goes into almost every building. When the boulders are gone it cuts Stone from cliffs and crags in reach, or digs it (resting while you have 40 Stone or more), so it never runs dry.
  4. Cement WorksNear your Command Base. It bakes Stone into Cement, your main building material (and roads). It stops at 40 Cement so it doesn't eat all your Stone; tap it to change the limit.
  5. Ore Mine and Crystal TapThe mine by the dark veins with glowing orange cracks, the tap by the violet crystals.
  6. SmelterTurns Ore and Fiber into Alloy, the backbone of everything advanced.
  7. Chip FabTurns Crystal and Alloy into Chips. Every new bot needs one.
  8. Bot FoundryBuilds the Workers and Warbots you order. Tap it and press +1 or +5. More workers means faster everything, so order a few early.
  9. More Fuel StationsOnce you're working far from your first one, so bots don't drive all the way back to recharge.
  10. Bio FarmBeside the dark, furrowed fertile soil. Fuel Stations burn Fuel, and the farm grows it. Your Agribot runs it.
  11. OutpostNear your border to claim more land, then run a road out to it and to everything you build there.

The supply chain

Cement is your main building material. Nearly every building costs Cement, and so does every road; a Cement Works bakes 2 Cement from each Stone. Only the Quarry (Fiber) and the Cement Works itself (raw Stone and Fiber) can be built without it, so you can always get going again.

Stock limits. Tap a production building to set when it stops: once you have that much of what it makes, it takes no more inputs and its worker goes off to other jobs until it's needed again. The Cement Works stops at 40, the Oil Pump and Refinery at 30, the Ammo Plant at 24, the Asphalt Plant at 20, the Chip Fab at 10 and the Missile Factory at 4 unless you change them, so Chips and Ammo don't eat all the Alloy your Foundry needs; the rest keep going until you set a limit. Gatherers and Bio Farms have no limit: pause them if their goods pile up.

Everything flows from four raw resources, plus energy crops and oil for Fuel. Resources sit in separate fields with open ground between them. Haulers carry goods two at a time along your roads, so short, fast roads and enough Haulers keep the chain moving.

Deposits run out, and your landing zone only has a little of everything. Stalk groves can be replanted with a Grove Planter (new groves take about a minute to grow). For more Ore, Crystal and oil, push your border out with Outposts: richer fields sit just beyond your starting land, and contested ones (including an oil field) lie in the middle of the map.

The Ring drizzle. In every game on the surface, Planetary events or not (not in the Missions, and not in the Hollow, where only Ringfall reaches, through the breaches), a lone piece of the planet's Ring streaks down every three to four minutes, the first about twelve minutes in, with a soft whoosh and a puff of dust. It harms nothing and lands only on open ground no colony holds, leaving one small rich deposit: 2 tiles of Ore or Crystal, each 40% as rich as a Ringfall deposit (in a Nuclear race, now and then a single tile of Uranium). A faint ring pulses on the minimap where it fell. Each colony gets one in its own share of the planet, the same distance from its base. Push your border out to claim them. Rivals look for reachable drops when their Ore or Crystal runs low. While 6 lie unclaimed, no more fall.

When a gatherer or Bio Farm has nothing left to work in reach (or an Oil Pump's seep runs dry), it shows its resource crossed out over it, frees its worker and tells you once. Dismantle it and build a new one by a fresh field: your haulers bring back all its materials.

Farmland can be burned for good. Napalm from a Vulcan Bomber, or a Torch's Scorch the earth, turns fertile soil to black char: the crops on it burn and no Bio Farm grows anything there again (soil under a building is spared). A farm whose fields burn makes less Fuel, and none once they're gone. A colony on Bio Farm Fuel alone is exposed; an Oil Pump and Refinery, or farms on more than one patch, keep the Fuel coming. The same goes for a rival that farms.

Buildings can stand side by side, but each needs one open side for bots and a road. Tap one of your buildings to set its priority, pause it or dismantle it. Dismantling takes it down and leaves everything that went into it in crates on its tile for your Haulers to bring back: all its materials, and anything inside it (a Silo's Missiles, a Nuclear Silo's missile as its parts, a Depot's stock). Cancelling a site leaves what was delivered for it the same way. A building the enemy destroys is simply gone: only what was inside it spills out, and a site loses what was delivered.

Robots and logistics

Your colony has two kinds of worker robot. Workers run buildings (one inside each production building), go out to cut, dig and mine resources for them, and build construction sites. Haulers move every good between buildings, and only on roads. The top bar shows your workers (and how many are free) and your haulers. The Bot Foundry builds more of both, and specialists: tap it and press +1 or +5 next to any type.

Rates and warnings on the top bar. On a computer each good's counter shows its net change a minute beside the count (green when it grows, red when it shrinks; rest the mouse on it for the figure). On a phone, tap the counter for the Goods panel and its rates. An amber ring on a counter means the buildings that make that good have their output stores nearly full: the haulers aren't keeping up, so add Haulers or a shorter road to storage. As you near the robot limit (90 in Versus, 150 in Colony) the robot counter turns amber and shows your count against it; at the limit the Bot Foundries stop until you recycle spare robots.

WorkerRuns buildings, gathers and builds. Goes anywhere, but crawls at 40% speed off the road, except while out gathering for its building.

HaulerCarries two goods at a time between buildings and storage. Only drives on roads: a building with no road gets nothing.

AgribotThe only bot that can run a Bio Farm or a Grove Planter.

PaverThe only bot that can run a Roadworks (two each). Lays planned roads twice as fast as a Worker, then asphalt.

Freight TruckCarries eight goods on the long runs between storage and to far-off buildings. Slower than a Hauler on concrete, fastest of all on asphalt. See Long hauls.

TankerThe only vehicle for Crude and Fuel, ten at a time. Explodes if it's destroyed with a load. See Tankers and pipelines.

Specialists have a small badge (amber crate, green leaf, yellow stripe) so you can tell them apart on the map. Every bot costs 1 Alloy and 1 Chip; a Freight Truck takes two rounds of parts, so 2 of each.

A delivery can use a different supplier when the nearest one lacks a suitable carrier. If a road or track is cut after pickup, the carrier keeps its cargo and looks for a detour or reachable storage.

Your robots: tap the robot counter in the top bar to see every robot you have by type (Workers, Haulers, Freight Trucks, Tankers, Agribots, Pavers, Warbots) and what they're doing: running buildings, carrying goods, building, recharging, or free; warbots in garrisons, in the field or out of Ammo. Show workers on the map rings every Worker in the colour of what it's doing (yellow for free ones, grey for those going to be recycled), with a line to where it's going, and dots them on the minimap; workers inside a building show as a count on it.

Recycling: every colony holds at most 90 robots (150 in Colony), and a robot you no longer need still fills a place. Tap it and press Recycle this unit: it walks or drives to the nearest Bot Foundry it can reach and is taken apart there, 2 seconds for each round of parts it took (it fades as it goes). Half of what it took to make comes back into the Foundry's stock for the colony to use (rounded down, Alloy first: 1 Alloy for a Worker or Warbot, 1 Alloy and 1 Chip for a Freight Truck, Tanker or Torch), and its place is free again. On the way it says Going to be recycled, at the Foundry Being taken apart; Keep this unit, or any other order, calls it off, and if the Foundry is wrecked first it just stops. Selected warbots have Recycle these N units on the selection bar (tap it twice). Your robots counts your spare Workers, those past one for each building that takes a Worker and four kept free for building, and Recycle spare Workers sends them all (never one running a building). Planes, ships, Freight Trains and Maglev Pods can't reach a Foundry, so they can't be recycled. A rival does the same with its own spare robots (Workers, Haulers, Freight Trucks, Tankers, Road Trains, Agribots; never warbots, nor a robot working, carrying goods, fighting or riding a ferry). Spare Workers must be idle for two minutes, or one minute during a parts shortage. Rivals recycle spares when the limit leaves no room for the army it wants, when it runs out of Alloy or Chips, and (from Medium up) when one has stood idle for minutes while its Foundry waits for parts.

Call a worker. A building that needs a Worker has Call a worker in its panel. It checks for a Worker who can actually reach the building. A free Worker is preferred, and the button can also draw one away from a job. If none can come, the panel tells you why.

  • Keep a worker or two free to build. If raids or new buildings leave none, the game takes a worker off the least useful job and tells you. The fix is more workers from the Bot Foundry.
  • Enough Haulers. If goods pile up waiting for pickup, order more Haulers. If every Hauler is lost, workers carry goods slowly until the Foundry makes more.
  • Priority. Tap a building or site and choose Low, Normal or High. High-priority ones get workers, builders and deliveries first. Low ones are served last and lose their worker first when you run short. A badge on the map shows anything you've changed. A priority set on a construction site is just for building it: once built, the building goes back to Normal.
  • Pause a building to free its worker until you resume it. A Generator, Mortar Pit or Pulse Turret has Turn off instead: it burns no Fuel, or holds its fire and its Ammo, and nothing more is brought to it until it's back on.
  • Undo a building you've just placed in the wrong spot: for 10 seconds an Undo chip shows at the foot of the map (on a computer, Ctrl+Z or ⌘Z too, outside Paths). It takes the site back with everything refunded in full: what was delivered or paid into it, and a Hauler's load on its way there, goes straight back into storage, with no crates left behind. Tap again for the site before it, while its 10 seconds last.
  • Blueprints: keep a block you like (a Smelter with its Mine and Depot) and put it down again elsewhere in one go. Build → Blueprints → New blueprint, tap your buildings and Save blueprint (on a computer you can also drag a box round them on the map and click Save as blueprint); your roads within a tile of them come too. Up to 8 stay on the device, named after what they're mostly made of (tap a name to change it). Place shows a ghost that follows the mouse or goes where you tap, Rotate (R) turns it, red marks what can't go and red dashes what the six-site limit holds back; Place here plans every site and road that fits at once and says how many went down.
  • Move a building you put in the wrong spot: tap Move, then the new spot. It's put together there for free in its build time, the old one keeps working meanwhile, and the new one takes over its goods and settings when it's ready. (Not the Command Base or Outposts: they hold your land, so build a new Outpost and dismantle the old one.)
  • Storage. Tap your Command Base or a Depot. Fill first makes haulers bring finished goods there before any other storage, even a closer one. Empty out takes nothing new and moves what it holds elsewhere, which is handy before dismantling it. Tap individual goods to stop that building keeping them.

Roads and traffic

Roads are the heart of your colony. Haulers only drive on roads, so every building and construction site needs a road joined to storage: the ring of road your Command Base sits on, or a Depot on the same network. A red road badge marks any building that has none, and the goal banner warns you. Workers can cross open ground, but slowly. Roads double every bot's speed.

Tap Paths and sketch a road: drag across open ground your bots can reach (yours or no-one's, never a rival's), or tap tiles to add them (tap one again to drop it). The sketch shows in bright blue, and the banner, which sits at the top of the screen so it never covers where you're drawing, shows what it will cost. Nothing is spent until you tap Build: a stray swipe costs nothing, Undo takes back the last drag and Clear wipes the sketch. Tap an existing road or a planned tile to mark it with a red cross to take up; it comes up when you tap Build, and its Cement comes back. To call off a whole stretch planned by mistake, drag along it from one of its tiles: every planned tile you pass is marked (a drag from planned road out onto open ground carries the road on instead, and built road is never touched by a drag). Track works the same. Short of Cement or Alloy? Plan it anyway: what you can't pay for yet waits (an orange outline) and is paid from storage as the goods come in, oldest plans first. Once it's built, Paths closes and you're straight back on the map; leaving Paths any other way drops an unbuilt sketch. On a computer, B then R (or P) opens it, and the build menu has a Roads card too.

Building pays the Cement at once: 1 Cement paves 5 tiles, and the same Cement builds your buildings, so every highway is a trade-off. Planned tiles show dashed until free workers pave them, about a second and a half per tile (three through resources, which they clear), always from the end nearest the roads you already have, so a new road is usable as it grows (a bridge or tunnel is built from your side); where its far end touches your roads or a finished building it grows from there too. Road crew: no more than 3 workers lay road at once, so the rest stay free for building; set it to 2, 3, 5 or No limit on the Roads tool in Paths. A worker laying road leaves it for a construction site nearby that needs a builder. A Roadworks' two Pavers lay planned road too, twice as fast, before any asphalt, and don't count toward the crew. A worker checks it has the charge to walk out, pave and get back to a Fuel Station before it sets off, and charges first if not. An oil seep isn't cleared: it stays underground under the road, ready to pump once the road is taken up.

Dragging goes round resources; tap a boulder, grove, ore or crystal tile to clear it for a road at three times the Cement. Every building keeps a side open for a road, and roads work on slopes too. An Outpost can stand right on a road: the road runs through it, but like a Gate only for your own bots. It never slows them down: it counts as the best road leading into it, so with asphalt on either side it's asphalt too. Enemies can't walk through your Outposts, nor you through theirs: an armed warbot has to shoot its way in, and that captures it. So an Outpost the enemy takes cuts the road through it: everything beyond it is cut off from your storage (its panel says No road to storage yet) until you take it back or lay a road round it. Bots follow a road round its corners rather than cutting across the ground. Plan long, straight highways between your fields, factories and Depots: it's how goods move over long distances efficiently. A long highway is a project, so plan it early and keep a few workers free to pave it.

  • Queues. Bots don't drive through each other on roads: each waits behind the bot ahead of it going the same way. A slow bot (say, one with a flat battery) backs up everyone behind it, and so does a crowd at a busy building.
  • Two-way roads. Each direction keeps to its own side, so bots can pass, but passing oncoming traffic slows both down. A busy two-way road between storage and a factory backs up.
  • One-way roads. In the Paths banner, choose Arrows and drag along a road: each tile points the way you drag. Tap a road to turn its arrow (east, south, west, north, then two-way again). Undo turns them back, a drag or a tap at a time; a drag that starts off your roads moves the map instead. Choose Lay roads and drag over arrows to clear them.
  • Loops. Lay two one-way lanes side by side, or a ring around a busy area, so bots heading out and bots coming back never share a lane.
  • Bots can still go the wrong way up a one-way road if there's no other route, but only at a crawl, so they plan around it. A bot stuck in a solid jam for a few seconds squeezes past, so traffic never locks up for good.

Paving and road wear

In the Paths banner choose Pave (E) and drag along roads to mark them for asphalt; drag starting on marked road to clear the marks. Marking is free. Your Roadworks pave the marked roads joined to them, nearest first, however far along the roads they are. Tap a Roadworks to switch it to Every joined road if you'd rather it surfaced everything it can reach.

Asphalt wears as traffic drives over it, and heavy Freight Trucks wear it three times as fast as other bots. Worn asphalt fades, cracks and breaks into potholes; past half worn it slows bots a little, and worn right through it drops back to plain road. The Paver goes back and resurfaces marked roads once they're half worn, which on a busy trunk road is every 8 to 10 minutes. So a Roadworks is a maintenance depot: put one near the highways that matter, and keep it supplied with Asphalt. Tap any of your roads to see its surface and wear, and mark it from there.

Bridges and tunnels

Drag a road out over water and it carries on as a bridge, and through rocky crags as a tunnel: there's no need to switch tools (the banner shows what each tile costs as you drag). The Bridges tool in Paths plans them too: over water you plan a bridge (2 Cement and half an Alloy a tile), through rock a tunnel (3 Cement and 1 Alloy). Free workers build them out from the shore or rock face, 6 seconds a tile for a bridge and 8 for a tunnel, and haulers can use each tile as soon as it's done. Bridges and tunnels join land on any level, so they can also take a road past a cliff. A bridge keeps one level all the way across, at the height of the highest clifftop it lands on, and ramps down onto a lower far side. To cross a dry valley or canyon between two clifftops, build a Viaduct (Paths → Viaducts). Lay road only uses them when going round would be much longer. Nothing is spent until you tap Build, so a stray drag through a crag costs nothing.

A bridge can also reach out over the water past your border, a tile at a time from your own shore, your own road or the end of a bridge. That's how you cross a channel the enemy's border covers: an assault bridge that lands your warbots on their shore. Where a bridge comes ashore on no-one's land, the ground within 3 tiles of the landing becomes yours, like a Viaduct's far end, so you can put an Outpost on the new island and keep going.

Driving. Truck exhaust and brake lights stay subtle in heavy traffic, and air brakes play one truck at a time. Bots speed up and slow down like real vehicles: they accelerate away from a stop, brake for corners, junctions and red lights, and pull up gently at the end of a trip. Every stop costs time, so straight roads, one-way loops, lights and overpasses pay off, most of all for Freight Trucks, which are slow to get going.

Junctions, traffic lights and overpasses

Where three or four roads meet, bots slow down to cross and give way to anyone already crossing the other way, so a busy crossing becomes a bottleneck. In the Paths banner choose Junctions (junctions are outlined in green) and tap one to put up a traffic light for 1 Alloy. It lets one direction through at a time at full speed and switches when traffic waits the other way. On a quiet crossing it costs a little (about 5%); where two busy roads cross it moves 50 to 85% more. An overpass beats both where two through roads cross, more than twice as much on a jammed crossing. Short of Alloy? Tap anyway: it's planned (a dashed orange marker) and goes up once it's paid for from storage.

Tap the light again to upgrade it to an overpass (4 Cement and 3 Alloy, less the light's Alloy, which comes back): the east-west road rises over the other on a short deck, so nobody slows down or waits at all. But it's two roads crossing, not a junction any more: nobody turns on an overpass. Traffic on each road goes straight through, and a bot that needs to turn goes round by another junction; if there isn't one, the two roads aren't joined there at all (a building on one road isn't linked to storage on the other through it). So build one where two busy roads cross and most traffic goes straight on, with a junction nearby for whoever turns. Overpasses only go where four roads cross: on a T-junction one road would end under the deck, so there the second tap takes the light down and says why, and an overpass left on one (if a crossing loses a road) turns back into a traffic light. Tap an overpass to take it down and get everything back. Tap one of your roads outside Paths to check: an overpass says straight through only, no turning.

Express highways

The road tier above asphalt, for the long hauls late in a game, with blank green sign gantries over its straights. In Paths choose Express (Shift+E, beside Pave) and drag: the tiles under your finger become a lane going the way you drag and the lane on their left comes back (bots drive on the right), with a median barrier, the street lamps and a strip in your colour between them at night; Shift and drag (or the banner's Single lane) lays one lane for a one-way loop. Over your own roads it upgrades them in place; elsewhere free Workers lay a road base (0.2 Cement a tile), then a Roadworks' Pavers lay the surface nearest first, 1 Asphalt and a quarter Alloy a tile from its stock (a 20-tile pair is 40 Asphalt and 10 Alloy). Freight Trucks and Tankers run on it at 4½× (asphalt 2¾×), Road Trains at 5½×, warbots at asphalt speed; Haulers, Workers, Agribots and Crawlers never drive along one and cross only at an overpass, so keep an ordinary road for them. Trucks keep their acceleration, so under about 8 tiles a lane gains them under a second. It wears like asphalt and is resurfaced the same way; worn through it drops to asphalt and its cat's-eyes go dark. A road touching a lane's side is a ramp (a junction, so bots give way); a lane ending on a road narrows onto it; where a road crosses a lane an overpass (4 Cement, 3 Alloy; two for a pair) is planned for you, and lanes never cross each other. Rivals build none yet; raiders tear a lane up like any road.

Route to

In Paths, on Roads, Bridges or Rail, tap Route to (or press Z) and tap a spot on the map: the cheapest road there from your road network (or track from your track or a Rail Station) is planned for you, round resource fields where it can, up one-level cliffs with a Ramp, over water as a bridge or through rock as a tunnel only when that saves a long way round, and with few bends. The banner says what it comes to; tap another spot to try a different one (it replaces the last), or tap Build to keep it. With a mouse, a dashed line shows the route to wherever the pointer is before you click. Drag from one spot to another to route between them instead.

Traffic map

In Paths, tap Traffic map (or press T) to tint every road by how much traffic it carried over the last minute, from green to red. Pulsing red rings mark the spots where bots stand waiting, the places to fix first: add a second lane, make a loop one-way, put a light or an overpass on the crossing, or move a busy building off the main road. The banner also shows how many of your Haulers and Freight Trucks are busy: if they all are, order more.

Goods flow

Beside it, Goods flow (or Shift+T) shows what your roads carry instead of how crowded they are. Every road and track tile your robots carried goods over in the last minute gets a line, wider the more goods went over it (the widest means 25 or more a minute) and coloured by the good most of them were, in the same colours as the counters in the top bar. A dashed ring in a good's colour marks a factory standing idle waiting for that good, and a red edge on a line a jam. Read it like this: a ring with no line of its colour leading to it is a starved chain (more of that good, or a road to it, needed); a wide line with a red edge is an overloaded road (a second road, a one-way loop or a light). The banner lists the goods on the move, most first.

Long hauls

The further your colony reaches, the longer every trip from the Command Base gets. These turn a long road into a real supply line:

  • Supply orders. Build a Depot near the front, tap it and use +5 to set how much of each good to keep there: Cement for building, Fuel for Fuel Stations, Ammo for the Outposts. More goods shows the rest. Goods are brought from your other storage (never below another Depot's own orders), and construction sites, factories, chargers and garrisons near the Depot then draw on it instead of waiting on the long trip home.
  • Freight Trucks. Order them at the Bot Foundry (2 Alloy, 2 Chips). A truck carries eight goods and takes the long runs, 12 tiles or more, from any building: a far mine's output to storage, storage to a far factory or garrison (which gets a bigger delivery, up to 8 of each input), and storage to storage. Haulers carry two and keep to the short runs (a Hauler just finishing a delivery takes the next short job beside it, so they don't drive across the colony empty): with trucks around they only take a long run when the building at the far end has run dry and no truck is free, and when every Hauler is busy an idle truck takes a short truckload too. A truck that delivers to a far building takes home what waits in storage beside it.
  • Trunk runs. A Depot 12 or more tiles from your Command Base sends home, a full load at a time, the goods nothing near it uses (keeping its supply orders and 4 of each), by truck or Road Train, never by Hauler. Build a Depot beside a far mining field: Haulers fill it, trucks bring it home in bulk, Haulers deliver locally at the other end.
  • Road Trains. A truck pulling two trailers: 30 goods (4 Alloy, 4 Chips). It only runs between your Command Base and far Depots (what a far Depot stocks by itself, supply orders and trunk runs), with 18 goods or more, several kinds in one trip. A far Depot stocks itself once a Road Train can drive to it (12 or more tiles from your Command Base): it keeps what the buildings round it need, the Road Train brings it, and Haulers do the short hop; tap the Depot to see what it keeps, or switch Auto supply off. If it runs out, Freight Trucks fill in straight to the buildings. Freight Trucks leave those loads for it (a far Depot's spare goods build up to a train load, and a big supply order waits for one), and whatever it delivers to, it takes that Depot's waiting goods home on the way back, as Freight Trucks do too. On a straight asphalt highway one Road Train stocking a far Depot does about the work of three Freight Trucks, on less Fuel; on plain road it crawls (35% speed) and does no better, wears the road hard and holds up the traffic behind it.
  • Ropeways. Paths → Ropeways (Shift + Y): tap the near station by your roads, then the far end, up to 14 tiles away, straight across cliffs, water or rock (8 Cement, 6 Alloy). Once the near station is built the cable goes across. Haulers, Workers and warbots ride the gondolas as part of your road network, so a cut-off plateau can be built on and supplied. Trucks are too heavy and keep to the roads.
  • Maglev lines. Build a Maglev Terminal (Build → Move → Maglev Terminal, G; 8 Cement, 6 Alloy, 3 Chips) by your roads, then Paths → Maglev (Shift + M, or Plan a line from here on its panel): tap the Terminal, then the far end 6 to 40 tiles away in any direction, a clear spot (the far Terminal goes up there) or another Terminal of yours. The preview rings every pylon spot (every 3 tiles): green a pylon, blue one over a road or track (its feet at the side; the road stays open), yellow one shifted a tile round an obstacle, red with a cross where nothing can stand (the reason beside it). A far end on a road or building moves to a good tile beside it, a line can bend at up to 4 pylons (tap a spot on the way, Bend here; each stretch 3 to 40 tiles, 60 in all), and where no straight line can go (or it's over 40 tiles) the tool offers Route with a bend, else Route via a hub: two lines through a new Terminal on the way. Pods follow the bends, slower round a sharp one. Edit bends on a Terminal's panel moves, adds or removes the bends of a built line: a job paid only for the length and pylons added, the old line running until it's done, what's taken down back as crates. 1 Alloy, half a Chip and 1 Cement a tile (plus a new far Terminal's cost), paid from storage as the goods come in; a Worker at the near Terminal builds it out pylon by pylon, the far Terminal last. Maglev Pods are built only at a Terminal's Pod bay (4 Alloy, 2 Chips each; four for each line): 16 goods a trip, several kinds, both ways, at three times a Freight Train's speed, no riders. The Terminals work like a railway's stations (the far one stocks what the buildings within 9 tiles need and sends their spare goods home; buildings touching a Terminal are joined by conveyor). Each Terminal needs a Generator with Fuel within 4 tiles, or the pods crawl at a Worker's pace (in a solar flare too), and the guideway's lights dim. Pylons are as tough as a Wall but any gun can bring one down: the line is cut, a pod on that span falls with its load, the rest turn back and wait, and a Worker from the nearer Terminal rides out to rebuild it in about 20 seconds. A Terminal joins up to three lines (a hub: tap one already on a line as the near end, or Add a line from here (hub) on its panel): lines joined at hubs make a network, and pods carry on through a hub without stopping, keeping aboard what the Terminals beyond it want. Terminals of two lines side by side also pass freight by conveyor. If a Terminal falls, its lines stay standing but broken (the pylons too, the pods safe at the other ends) until Rebuild the missing Terminal on a surviving Terminal's panel puts a new one on its tile at half a Terminal's cost (on the old foundation): every line that ended there runs again. Allowed in the Hollow.

Build a Mass Driver (Build → Move, V; 10 Cement, 8 Alloy, 4 Chips; two tiles long, R turns it east-west or north-south as you place it; it fires off the end facing its Catcher) and a Catcher (C; 6 Cement, 4 Alloy, 2 Chips) 12 to 30 tiles away. A canister of 12 goods flies every 10 seconds in an arc over everything, about 3 seconds in the air at full range, and lands in the Catcher, whose stock is shared out like a Depot's. It burns no Fuel but fires only with a Generator within 4 tiles. An enemy Flak Gun can burst a canister in flight, and one with no Catcher left lands anyway: either way its goods fall as crates. While it charges the coils light blue-white in sequence with arcs crackling up the rail; a faint guidance beam reaches toward the Catcher as the canister flies. Not in the Hollow.
  • Viaducts. Paths → Viaducts (Shift + V): tap a clifftop by your roads, then a highlighted far end across a valley or canyon, in a straight line up to 12 tiles away and no more than 2 levels up or down (on Sky islands, mesa top to mesa top). 3 Cement for every tile spanned and 1 Alloy for every two, paid from storage as the goods come in; a worker builds it out from the near end. Everything drives across, trucks and Road Trains included (and trains, with track on both ends), while traffic down below carries on underneath. The far end can land on no-one's land or even in a rival's, as a bridgehead for an attack. Warbots on a clifftop shoot across a narrow canyon at enemies on the other side. A Vulcan Bomber can bomb its deck: two bombs bring it down.
  • Airfields and air drops. An Airfield (Build → Move; 10 Cement, 4 Alloy) needs a 5-tile runway of open, level ground in a straight line from it. A Cargo Plane from its hangar (one per Airfield) drops one load a flight by parachute: a Drop Outpost anywhere nobody holds (a foothold: a little land, 2 warbots, storage, charging), 2 Warbots or 2 Workers anywhere, or 8 goods for a place you supply by air (any building, sites too; a site you place in the land of a place supplied by air is too). Its supply orders then come by plane only, not by Hauler, until a road from home reaches the place: then Supply by air switches itself off and Haulers take over (where a road already reaches, it can't be switched on). A Drop Outpost also gets what the buildings near it need, and Workers carry it over by hand. Its Workers build what you place round it: a Worker isn't called out from far away while one close by will soon be free. Drop an outpost behind the rival's base, drop Workers, plan a Missile Silo there, and the plane flies in its materials and then its Missiles. It rolls down the runway to take off and lands back on it to refuel with Fuel (planes, like trucks and ships, have a Fuel tank, not a battery). Flying is thirsty: a full tank lasts about 45 seconds in the air, a Cargo Plane's big tank takes 5 Fuel to fill, and a long round trip costs it 4 to 6 Fuel. Each of your planes shows a pink fuel gauge over it, and in its panel. The Airfield keeps 12 Fuel for its planes (Tankers bring it), and its panel shows what it holds and about how many flights that covers; a plane checks its range before it takes off and waits, saying so, until the Fuel for the trip is there. Watch for Flak Guns (Build → Defend): they fill the sky round aircraft with bursting shells out to 7 tiles; one quick pass gets through hurt, a few guns bring a plane down, and the rival builds them once you have an Airfield.
  • Airships and Mooring Masts. Build two Mooring Masts (Build → Move → Mooring Mast, T; 6 Cement, 3 Alloy; at least 8 tiles apart), tap one, press Link to another Mast (L) and tap the other: that's an Airship line, straight over water, cliffs, rock and canyons (Unlink takes it down; a Mast is on one line at most, and the line shows dashed while a Mast or its Airship is selected). An Airship comes from an Airfield's hangar (Hangar: Airships +1; nine rounds of Alloy and Chips) but takes no spot there: it flies to its line and lives at its Masts. With exactly one line it goes there by itself; otherwise pick the line on its panel. Two to a line. It carries up to 24 goods a trip, several kinds, both ways, at half a Cargo Plane's speed: the Masts work like a railway's stations, the far one stocking what the buildings round it need and sending their spare goods home, the one nearer home keeping that ready (Fuel and Crude fly too). Your Workers and Warbots ride it whenever it's the shorter way, six at a time, like a ferry. It burns Fuel in the air and fills up at either Mast; each keeps 8 for it, brought by Tanker or by pipe (Pipe Fuel to it on a Mast's panel; a piped Mast takes only the Fuel it keeps, gives none out, and passes the rest on along the pipe). Moored, it hangs nose-first at the Mast's cup, its body along the line; tap it to see its hold, riders, Fuel and line. It's unarmed, with 40 hit points: Flak brings it down with all aboard, so keep its line clear of a rival's guns.
  • Vulcan Bombers (Versus): an Airfield's hangar builds them (two fit, beside its Cargo Plane; five rounds of Alloy and Chips). Parked, one plane waits at the head of the runway, lined up to roll, and the rest are in the hangar (a tag on the Airfield says how many). Tap an Airfield and press Strike by a bomber, or open a rival building and tap Bomb it (Z), or tap the bomber and Pick a target: an enemy building, or a truck, Tanker, ship or robot out in the open, which it chases, or the deck of an enemy Viaduct (the bomb falls where you tapped and takes 14 off the end nearer it, or off the weaker end; an end has 24 armor, so two bombs anywhere on the deck bring the span down, one end falling taking the other). It loads 6 Ammo at its Airfield (the Airfield keeps some for its bombers; haulers bring it) and 3 Fuel on top of the Fuel its tank needs for the trip (it can't take off without them, and its panel says what it's waiting for), flies out, drops one heavy bomb (14 off a building, Outposts and Bunkers included; only a Command Base or Keep it can't knock below a third of its armor; it hurts what's round it, and a Tanker it hits explodes) and flies home, where it needs 25 seconds to rearm. Tap an enemy building, vehicle or robot out in the open and press Bomb it to send one. Two bombers sent at one target don't waste a bomb: if the first one's bomb will finish it, the second brings its bomb home (its Ammo goes back into the Airfield, only the Fuel is spent) and needs no rearming. A full tank sets how far it reaches. Flak Guns are the answer: a few of them shoot it down. A rival other than a Warden builds an Airfield late in a war, with one more Bio Farm or Refinery to keep its planes in Fuel, and sends a bomber now and then at something valuable that no Flak covers. Changed your mind while it waits on the ground for Ammo or Fuel? Cancel strike by the bomber calls it off.
  • Napalm. Picking a bomber's target, the banner's Payload switch chooses Bomb (B) or Napalm (N); a rival building's panel has Napalm beside Bomb it, and any patch of fertile soil or crops Napalm it while you have a Vulcan Bomber (on your own farmland it reads Napalm your own farmland; greyed out, it says why). Napalm takes no Ammo but 3 more Fuel at the Airfield (6 on top of its tank in all), and it can be aimed at any ground in reach. The tile it lands on and the eight round it burn for 6 seconds, hurting enemy robots and buildings there a little, and every patch of fertile soil in it is scorched for good, crops and all: no Bio Farm grows anything there again. While you aim, the soil it would burn shows in amber. A rival that lives on Bio Farm Fuel (two farms or more and no Oil Pump) may napalm yours too, every eight minutes at most.
  • Shot down. An aircraft Flak brings down falls burning for several seconds (a Cargo Plane about 7½, a Vulcan Bomber 6½, an Airship 11, settling slowly), carried on in a falling turn and trailing thick smoke, pieces breaking off, then crashes in a fireball. Nothing it lands on is hurt. Its burnt-out wreck stays where it came down for the rest of the game, slowly overgrown, carrion birds circling it while it's new; at night a few small flames and a blinking red light show where it lies. It's only scenery: robots drive over it, and a building, road or track built over it clears it away (so does the tide). Up to 32 stay; past that the oldest go.
  • Ships and Ports (naval maps: Islands and Lakes). A Port (Build → Move; 8 Cement, 3 Alloy) goes on the shore beside open water and works as storage. Ships are built at a Port's shipyard: tap the Port and order them, haulers bring it Alloy and Chips, and each one puts to sea from that Port. Container Ships (40 goods) and Tanker Ships (40 Crude or Fuel) fill a far Port's supply orders from your other Ports on the same water, and carry its spare goods home. A Ferry runs a ferry line between two Ports (set it in a Port's panel): your robots and trucks take it whenever it's the shorter way. A Ferry can also make a beach landing: up to six warbots, onto any shore it can sail to, the rival's too. Ships burn Fuel and fill up at a Port (each Port keeps a little for them); turrets, Mortar Pits and Missiles can sink them. Long bridges cost more the further they reach from shore on these maps.
  • Footholds on other islands. You can build on any island where you hold something: an Outpost, a Drop Outpost a Cargo Plane dropped, or a Port. A Ferry can found a Port on another shore (on its panel, or a Port's): it loads 12 Cement and 4 Alloy at its Port (haulers bring them) and up to 4 idle Workers, sails to an empty shore nobody holds and puts the Port up there, claiming the land 4 tiles round it, then heads home running a ferry line to it. An island your ferry line reaches is joined to home: Haulers, Workers and trucks cross on the Ferry to work there. A Ferry tied up at a Port is inside it: tap the Port and use the button for it in Ships, then Sail out of the Port. Tap a Ferry to take charge of it: Sail somewhere (open water, right up to a shore, or one of your Ports; with a mouse, right-click the water while its panel is open), Take warbots aboard from the beach beside it (with a mouse, select warbots and right-click the Ferry: they walk over and board), Put them ashore beside it (then it heads home by itself), or send it Back to its line.
  • Freight rail (optional). Paths → Rail (N): drag to lay track on your land or no-one's, 1.5 Cement and a quarter of an Alloy a tile, laid as soon as you confirm (or as the goods come in). Track climbs one level at a time over a Ramp, like a road: drag it across a one-level cliff and a Ramp is planned for it automatically, with the track going down over it once it's built; trains slow on the grade. Over water the track becomes a rail bridge (3 Cement and half an Alloy a tile), and through rocky crags a rail tunnel (3 Cement and 1 Alloy): the drag carries straight on into both; across a dry valley or canyon, build a Viaduct and lay track onto both its ends. Put a Rail Station (6 Cement, 2 Alloy) at each end of a line, beside the track, or first, on bare ground: then drag rail from one station to the other and the track is laid up to each, ending beside it. Freight Trains are built at a station's rail yard: tap the station and order them, haulers bring it Alloy and Chips (five rounds of parts), and each one rolls out onto that station's track. The Bot Foundry never needs to be near the railway. Freight Trains (five rounds of parts) carry 56 goods, several kinds a trip, between stations on the same track, fast and with no charging or Fuel. They run the line on their own (each station's Auto supply, switchable on its panel): a far station stocks what the buildings round it need, the station nearest your Command Base keeps what the far stations want, a train sets out once its load is worth the trip (sooner if the far station runs low) and brings the far station's spare goods home on the way back; trucks leave the buildings a far station serves to the train. Supply orders you set come on top. Station conveyors: a factory, a site, your Command Base or a Depot touching a station is joined to it by conveyor, no Hauler needed, so build the home station beside your Command Base and your far factories round the far station. Road bots wait at level crossings while a train passes. Armored trains: tap a train (or the button for it on a Rail Station's panel, while it's stopped there) and press Armor this train (6 Alloy, 3 Chips, 3 Crystal, paid from storage there and then). It finishes its run and stops at a Rail Station, where the yard fits it in 12 seconds: plating that gives it 40 more hit points (70 in all) and takes 40% off any damage, a pulse cannon on the locomotive that shoots enemy robots and vehicles within 4.5 tiles, and a twin flak gun on the last wagon that fires at enemy aircraft within 6 tiles. Both run off the engine: no ammo to bring. The rival armors its trains too, once it has goods to spare.
  • Which carrier? Feeding four Smelters 36 tiles out (64 goods a minute in, 48 Alloy out): 8 Haulers alone, about 40% of full output; three Freight Trucks, 70% on plain road and about 90% on asphalt; one Road Train keeping a Depot there stocked by itself, on asphalt, about 90% on less Fuel (55% without the Depot stocking itself); a rail yard (stations touching the Command Base and the Smelters), 100% with no Hauler trips and no Fuel; a line whose stations touch nothing, 70% with 8 Haulers making the hop at each end (like the trucks) and 85% with 12. Haulers stay best under 12 tiles; rail costs the most to build.
  • Crawlers. A colossal tracked base (six rounds of parts). Tap it, Drive somewhere (no road needed) and it deploys where it stops into a Depot, a Fuel Station and an Outpost in one. Pack up to move takes its stored goods along.
  • Pave the trunk road. Trucks are heavy: on concrete they go at 80% of their speed, slower than a Hauler, but on asphalt they're the fastest thing on the map. A Roadworks along the highway pays for itself.
  • Charge on the way. A long trip drains batteries: put a Fuel Station by the forward Depot, and give the Depot a Fuel order to keep it running.
  • The frontier pays. Boulders, ore, crystal and oil hold more the further they are from the bases, up to 2.2 times as much in the middle of the map. Rich deposits sparkle, and their panel says so.

Batteries

Every bot runs on a battery that drains as it walks, works and fights. Bots recharge only at a Fuel Station (four at full speed): your Command Base doesn't charge anyone, and you land without one, so build your first Fuel Station straight away: it runs on the 10 Fuel you land with. Warbots also recharge inside Outposts, and a low warbot in your Command Base slips out to the nearest Outpost or Fuel Station for a top-up while all is quiet. When more bots crowd in than a charger has slots, they share the power and charge slower. A bot that needs charge goes to the Fuel Station where it will be charged soonest: a short wait at a busy one nearby beats a long walk to an empty one, and one that has run out of Fuel with more on its way is worth waiting for. A worker with nothing to do tops up only at one a short walk away.

Fuel

Fuel Stations burn Fuel: one Fuel recharges two full batteries, and each station keeps up to 4 in stock (workers deliver it). A station out of Fuel can't charge anyone, and its bots head for another charger. There are two ways to make Fuel:

  • Bio Farm. Build it within 3 tiles of fertile soil (dark, furrowed patches). Its worker plants energy crops, waits about 45 seconds for them to ripen, harvests each into one Fuel and replants. More fertile tiles in reach means steadier output, up to about 4 or 5: that's all one Agribot keeps up with, so one farm a patch does it, two on a big one (two crowded onto a small patch only share its soil). Crops never run out.
  • Oil. Build an Oil Pump right on top of an oil seep (a glossy black puddle of crude with a few droplets beside it; a pump goes on any of its tiles, and as it pumps the puddle shrinks away from it and breaks into pools, so you can see how much is left). It pumps Crude until the seep runs dry, and a Refinery turns each Crude into a Fuel: about 10 Fuel a minute, four times a Bio Farm, but a pump spot only holds 50 or so Crude before you have to move on. Seeps are few: one just beyond your border and a contested field in the middle of the map. When a seep runs dry its pump shows a crossed-out oil drop and frees its worker: dismantle it (its materials and any Crude left in it are left in crates for your Haulers).

Tankers and pipelines

Crude and Fuel are liquids: only Tankers carry them, ten at a time, to wherever they're needed, near or far. You land with one Tanker; order more at the Bot Foundry (2 Alloy, 2 Chips) as your Fuel Stations, farms and pumps grow. Haulers carry a liquid only when no Tanker can get there, one can at a time, so nothing ever stops for good. A Tanker brings a factory such as a Refinery a full load, so it isn't kept waiting.

Fuel is dangerous. A Tanker destroyed with a load on board explodes, hurting every robot and building round it (yours too), and a Refinery that's wrecked by warbots or a missile goes up in a bigger blast; the fuller, the bigger. A full Fuel Tank is the biggest of all, nearly 4 tiles round. Loaded Tankers, Refineries and Fuel Tanks caught in a blast go up a moment later. Keep your Refinery away from the front, and dismantle it rather than let it fall (dismantling is safe).

A neighbouring Refinery or Fuel Tank takes Crude directly. An Oil Pump offers Pipe it in when there is a new route to plan. Pipelines carry Crude straight from your Oil Pumps to a Refinery, every second, so pumped Crude never waits for a Tanker. They carry Fuel too: pipe a Refinery or Bio Farm, or your Command Base or a Depot, to your Fuel Stations and Generators and they stay topped up with no Tanker trips (tap one in Pipes, or Pipe Fuel to it on its panel, to pipe it to the nearest Fuel). A Fuel Station passes Fuel on down the line, so you can run one pipe past several. Open Paths and pick Pipes (K), then drag from a pump to a Refinery: the pipe finds its own way there inside your border, over roads and railway track, round buildings, up and down cliffs, and across water on stilts or bored through rock where that's the better way (1.2 Cement a tile there, against 0.3 on land). Or tap a pump to pipe it to the nearest Refinery or Fuel Tank, tap Pipe every Oil Pump, or use Pipe it in on a pump's panel. A pipeline with a Fuel Tank on it moves 8 a second instead of 4, and fills the Fuel Tank before other storage. Pipe costs 0.3 Cement a tile and goes down as soon as you tap Build. A pipe joins every building it runs beside, so a Depot or your Command Base on the line takes the Crude the Refinery can't (and feeds it back when it runs low). Raiders cut pipe and missiles break it; broken pipe is relaid once it's paid for again.

Trucks burn Fuel. Freight Trucks, Road Trains and Tankers have a fuel tank instead of a battery: it drains as they drive, and when it's low they fill up at any storage or Fuel Station holding Fuel (one Fuel fills the tank, in a moment). With no Fuel anywhere they crawl at half speed.

Feed links. Put a factory right beside the building that makes what it uses and a short conveyor joins them (a pipe for Crude and Fuel): goods go straight across, one every 2 seconds, with no Hauler, and the factory fed that way works a tenth faster. Ore Mine next to Smelter next to Chip Fab, a Quarry beside a Cement Works, Smelter and Cement Works round an Ammo Plant, or a Refinery, Asphalt Plant and Roadworks in a row (the Refinery passes its Crude along) all work as blocks. Each building feeds at most two neighbours, and storage never takes part, except a Fuel Tank: it feeds Crude to up to four Refineries and Asphalt Plants round it and takes in their Fuel, so pumps piped into a Fuel Tank with Refineries round it make an oil bloc. While you place a building, green arrows show the links it would make; its panel shows what it feeds and what feeds it. Packed blocks are efficient, but a missile or a fuel blast hurts everything in them at once. Its Keep at least setting keeps a minimum of Fuel and Crude in it: Tankers and Haulers top it up.

Trade routes: the Trade Terminal (8 Cement, 3 Alloy; Build, Move, keys 3 M): it docks at a neutral Trade Post: off-world exchanges standing on no-one's ground, run by a small neutral AI that still keeps the Accord's ledger: an octagonal deck with a tall spire ringed in glowing gold (gold diamonds on the minimap), placed fairly between the colonies when the planet is made (one more than there are colonies; two in Colony mode, about 24 tiles out). Dock your Terminal within 2 tiles of a post, one per post, and lay a road (or track) out to it: no need to be near your other buildings. Nobody owns a post, so a race for the nearest is on, and a rival's land round a post keeps you out: you can't dock or lay road on their ground, so an Outpost of yours beside a post shuts them out (tap a post to see who has docked there). A Terminal runs one standing trade route: on its panel tap a good under Send and one under Bring back, then Start route (G); it runs until you change it or Stop route (X). A second route needs a second Terminal at another post. The post's ledger takes one run from each Terminal every 2 minutes: a convoy, a Road Train (or a Freight Train with track beside the Terminal from a Rail Station), takes 15 of the good out from storage that holds 15, waits at the dock if it's early, and brings home what they bought (never Haulers or Freight Trucks). What a run bought can't be spent until it's home. Bought Fuel or Crude goes home by Tanker, or Fuel by a pipeline from the Terminal to storage. The panel's market board lists what each good sells for and costs on the ledger: raw goods (Stone, Fiber, Ore, Crystal, Crude) sell for about a third of their worth and cost three-quarters of it; made goods (Cement, Alloy, Chips, Ammo, Fuel) sell for a little over half and cost their worth, and Fuel costs half as much again. So 15 Stone brings back about 2 Alloy, 15 Cement about 2 Alloy, 15 Alloy about 17 Crystal, and 15 Stone about 2 Fuel. No route trades a raw good for another raw good or a made good for what it's made from (no Cement for Stone, no Alloy for Ore), Cement only buys made goods, and Crude and Fuel are only ever bought; a rich good goes out in fewer than 15 so what comes back fits the convoy. A post further out pays more: 1.5% for every tile beyond 16 from your Command Base (up to a quarter more). Each run you sell of a good knocks a tenth off what it fetches (down to half), and the price comes back about halfway in four minutes: one route on a good settles a little under full price, two drive it to the floor. It never beats making goods yourself: a route is a steady trickle all game. Nothing shoots on a Trade Post's ground (the gold dashed square, 3 tiles round the pad): no warbot, gun, Howitzer, bomb or Missile hits anything there and nothing there fires out (but warbots can wreck a rival's Terminal while it's still a construction site), and only the Trade Terminal (and roads, track and pipes) can be built on it (so no deposits lie there: a piece of Ringfall that comes down on it lies just off it), so the fights over trade happen out on the routes. Convoys fly a gold pennant, your routes are drawn dashed on the minimap with the convoys as gold dots, you hear the moment one is hit, and a destroyed convoy spills its cargo as crates for whoever gets there first. Warbots holding a rally flag go after an enemy convoy that passes within 6 tiles of the flag, and by a trade route they lie in wait instead of tearing that road up: plant one beside a rival's route (dashed on your minimap too), out past its land, to raid it. An unescorted convoy turns back from raiders like any hauler; an escorted one holds while its escort fights. Turn on Escort (E) and two armed warbots from the nearest garrison ride along with each convoy, get off to fight what comes at it (the convoy holds back while they do), and walk back once it's home. Every rival docks a Terminal from about 8 minutes in (an Architect sooner) and runs its own route, with Escort on: one short of Crystal routes for Crystal or Chips and never sends its Crystal away, one that has run dry of Fuel with no Bio Farm or oil to make it routes for Fuel, and a Salvager's favourite raid is the road an enemy's convoys run, out of reach of the guns. The Wren still brings its occasional big deals.

A Generator (Build, Make, keys 2 G) burns a Fuel every 40 seconds while something in its reach is working, and makes every factory, Refinery, Oil Pump and Bot Foundry within 4 tiles work a third faster. Put it in the middle of your industry. Tap it (or start placing one) to see its reach: an amber ring, with every building it powers marked by a lightning badge.

Terrain and territory

The planet has terraces at different heights: lighter ground is higher, and dark rock walls are cliffs. Bots can't climb cliffs. They use slopes (the striped ramps) at passes, so some routes wind a long way round.

  • Ramp: the easy way is to drag a road (or track) up a one-level cliff: a Ramp (3 Cement, 1 Fiber) is planned where it climbs, and it's part of the road once built. Or, in Paths, pick Ramps and tap the top or foot of a one-level cliff to open your own route up or down. Ramps are paid from storage as the goods come in and don't count against the construction-site limit; no Ramp can climb a cliff two levels high. Workers build it from whichever side of the cliff is the shorter trip, so a Ramp down off high ground is built from the top: nobody drives all the way round to the bottom, and the bottom can even be ground your bots can't reach yet. It counts as road once built. A pass is often worth a long paved Path too.
  • You can't place a building where it would block the only way through a pass, and resources across a cliff with no route don't count as "in reach".
  • Water and rocky crags block bots, but a bridge or tunnel (Paths, Bridges) takes a road across or through them.
  • Salvage: if you run out of Stone or Fiber with no Quarry or Harvester that can still work, your Command Base's crew salvages 1 every 20 seconds (up to 6), enough to build your way back. Until then it goes only to building sites, so a Smelter can't burn the Fiber your new Harvester needs.
  • Land and influence: the dashed line shows your sphere of influence. The Command Base holds 8 tiles around it, each Outpost 7 and each Keep 6, and every other building you finish holds the 2 tiles round itself, so your land grows as you build. You can build on your own land or on no-one's ground within 10 tiles of your buildings, roads or track; lay a road out and you can build along it. Never on a rival's land. Defences (Pulse Turrets, Bunkers, Disruptors, Mortar Pits, Flak, Keeps and Silos) only go inside your own border: an Outpost or Keep pushes it out. Walls and Gates go anywhere on no-one's land your bots can walk to (to fence off a pass, a field or a far road), but never on a rival's. Roads, track and pipes can run anywhere your bots can reach that no rival holds. A rival's roads, track and pipes inside your Command Base, Outpost or Keep ring become yours (yours to use, change or rip up), and you can rip up any road on land you hold.
  • Keeping clear of enemy guns and warbots: workers, haulers and trucks won't go where an enemy gun can shoot them: a loaded Pulse Turret, Minigun Nest or Keep cannon, a Mortar Pit with shells, or a Bunker or Keep with armed warbots inside. A building of yours one covers gets no worker, builder or deliveries (its panel says Under enemy fire, and anyone working there pulls out), gatherers pick spots out of reach, and routes go round where they can. Knock the gun out, or wait for it to run dry, and the work picks up again. The rival AI does the same, and sends warbots, Howitzers, a missile or a bomber to clear a gun covering its buildings. They keep clear of enemy warbots out in the open too (armed ones, and an armored train's cannon; in Colony, creatures): nobody is sent to work at a building with enemy warbots about (its panel says so), routes go round them, and a worker or hauler whose way runs into them stops short and turns back, keeping any load it carries. One they come up on runs into storage close by, or out of their reach, and gatherers wait inside their buildings. Once they've gone, or your warbots have driven them off, the work picks up again.
  • Cut off: an Outpost or Keep's wide ring outranks the little ring round an ordinary building. If a rival's ring takes the ground under one of your buildings (they put an Outpost close by, or yours that covered it fell), it's cut off: a red wash and a broken-link badge, it stops working, nothing is hauled to it, and it crumbles away over about a minute and a half, spilling what was inside. No-one can plant an Outpost or Keep whose ring would land on a rival's buildings, so this happens when a fort is captured or falls, not because one went up next door. Take the land back (an Outpost or Keep of yours nearer than theirs, or knock theirs down) and it's yours again, working as before. A construction site on ground a rival takes is lost at once. Gatherers harvest anywhere but a rival's land.

The Hollow

A map of its own

The Murmur's cavern lair under the Bloom, for two. Solid rock carved into caverns and winding passages, the same on both sides: your home cavern, side caverns, a chasm hall split by a chasm with one rock arch, a flank cavern on each side of the middle, and the cathedral in the middle where the Deep's mound stands. It's always night down there, whatever the Time of day setting, and it has its own look whatever the Biome. The Murmur setting starts at Active on this map.

  • Breaches: shafts of pale gold daylight down through the roof, the only daylight there is. Your Command Base lands under one; four more stand in the flank caverns, two each. Ringfall falls only through the breaches, in concentrated, richer drops (two strikes in three through the flank caverns').
  • Bloom veins: nothing grows without light, so no stalk groves, no fertile soil, no Grove Planters and no Bio Farms. A Harvester cuts Fiber from the violet veins on the cavern floors and along the walls instead; a vein cut down grows back in about 2½ minutes. Cutting them draws the Murmur: in the next surge their tunnels head for a Harvester at work on the veins, and the packs go for it first. Fuel comes from the oil seep in your home cavern (two on Small maps): an Oil Pump and a Refinery.
  • Floating Crystal: the richest Crystal (two and a half times a cluster's) on masses hanging over the chasms, raised high: one in each chasm hall and, from Medium up, one in each east cavern. Bots can't walk onto them. Run a Ropeway (Paths) out to one, or a Sky bridge or Viaduct across, then build a Crystal Tap on it.
  • Side chambers: robot parts laid out in rows: crates of Alloy and Chips that never rot, for warbots to carry home.
  • Ceiling tremors, the Hollow's weather: every two minutes or so the roof shakes and dust sifts down; every other time rock comes down somewhere near a colony. Dust trickles down there for 8 seconds first, with a ring round the spot: get robots clear. It dents buildings (never wrecks one: Repair it) and hurts robots, and leaves a boulder of Stone. No dust storms or solar flares underground.
  • No aircraft or missiles fly underground: no Airfields, Mooring Masts, Missile Factories or Missile Silos, and no Nuclear race (the Build menu greys them out). The black lakes are far too small for Ports.
  • No Trade Posts or Uplinks: the Pathfinder, which built them, never mapped the Hollow. No Trade Terminals or trade routes there, and the Uplinks victory isn't offered on this map (picked anyway, the game is won by Capture).
  • The Murmur: more nests than on any other planet, and the Deep's mound in the middle, the biggest of all: heavily armored and well guarded. Its own defense packs start below 75% armor and grow larger and more frequent below two-thirds and one-third. Watch for stronger violet pulses and listen for a deeper rumble with each burst. Wreck it and its hoard spills out, and every other nest falls back to its first level. The nests come in pairs, the same on both sides, and like everywhere else they tunnel toward your power and open exits. With no day down there, the nights are surges, on the same clock: the hum in the rock goes dead silent a minute before each one. On Quiet half the nests are there; on Off none (the menu warns you: this is their lair). Tunnel packs come in the surges. In Colony no more exits open at once than on a surface map of the same size.
  • Ore and oil run out: the Ore and oil seeps near home don't last a long game, and much of what lies further out sits within a few tiles of a nest, where its guards keep your workers off. Clear those nests between surges to mine and pump it. The computer rival does: once its own Ore or oil runs low, it goes first for the nests sitting on more. With parts scarce and a clear lead, a rival can gather a smaller armed force for the final push if it can beat the guns and garrison. At low Fuel, key industry Workers keep working if the available charge would not repay the trip. An Ore-starved rival can mine a single accessible vein and ignores distant, unclaimed veins when judging which nests to clear.

War

Versus & Colony free play

Warbots come from the Bot Foundry and wait inside your Command Base and Outposts, filling front-line Outposts first. When enemy warbots come close, a garrison sends defenders out automatically. Buildings don't mend on their own: tap one and press Repair.

Ways to win

Pick how a Versus game is won with Victory on the new-game screen:

  • Capture (the usual): take a rival's Command Base and everything it built is yours; the last colony standing wins.
  • Domination: hold most of the land for 3 minutes straight. A card under the top bar shows each colony's share of all the land anyone holds, with a mark where Domination starts: 60% with one rival, half with two, 45% with three (and you need a fifth of the whole planet too). Whoever holds that much starts a 3-minute count, with warnings at a minute and at 20 seconds; slip under and it starts again. Outposts and Keeps claim the most land, so build them out, and knock down the rival's to break its hold: the rivals go for the Outposts of whoever is counting down.
  • Uplinks: neutral relay towers stand across the planet, 3 to 5 by the map, in fair spots for every colony (ringed dots on the minimap). An Uplink is yours while it stands in your land, so build an Outpost or a Keep near it (a Drop Outpost will do, or a Port across the water). They come online 8 minutes in (the card counts down): until then you can claim them, but no clock runs. From then on, hold at least half of them and more than anyone else (2 of 3; 2 of 4 while the others hold fewer; 3 of 5) and your clock runs; a total of 5 minutes on it wins. Lose the lead (a tie is no lead) and your clock stops but keeps its time. Everyone is warned when a clock has a minute and 20 seconds to go. Armed enemy warbots standing right beside an Uplink jam it: it counts for no one while they're there. A card under the top bar shows how many Uplinks each colony holds and everyone's time; tap the card to look at each Uplink in turn, and tap an Uplink for its sheet (Hold it with warbots plants a hold flag beside it). The rivals play for the Uplinks too, not your Command Base: they race you for the free ones (bridging the sea or a canyon to reach one), keep guards and a Bunker beside each one they hold, and send their army, bombers and missiles at the Outposts holding yours, jamming them while your clock runs.

Surrender: whichever way you play, a rival that has clearly lost gives up rather than make you dig out its last buildings one by one. Twelve minutes or more in, once its colony is down to 40% of what it was at its height and yours is four times as strong (buildings, and warbots counting double), or it has nothing left to rebuild with (no Bot Foundry, no Workers, next to no army), it surrenders after about 40 seconds of that and everything it had is yours, as if you'd captured its Command Base.

Keep playing: won against the computer and enjoying it? Keep playing on the victory screen carries on with the same map: the rivals still standing fight on and no longer surrender, nothing wins again, and only leaving (or losing your own colony) ends it. Not in multiplayer or missions.

Free-for-all

With three or four players, the red, green and purple rivals fight each other as well as you, each going after whoever is nearest. Capture a rival's Command Base and everything it built becomes yours: its factories, Outposts, turrets and roads, though its robots shut down. Watch for one rival swallowing the other: it comes out twice the size. The last colony standing wins; if your own Command Base falls, the game is over.

Four players (You vs 3, on Crossroads, Pinwheel, Four Isles or Twin Valleys): a square planet in four quarters, a colony in each corner. You start in the south-west, the red rival (VERMILION) north of you, the purple one (TYRIAN) east of you, and the green one (VIRIDIAN) across the plaza. Each quarter is the same, turned. On Crossroads your two neighbours' mesas lie across the lowland below yours (a slope down off your mesa faces each of them, and a third the plaza in the middle); on Pinwheel they are a ridge away, round its outer end or through the plaza. The green colony is the far one. With You vs 2 on Crossroads, Pinwheel or Four Isles, the quarter beside you to the north is left free and rich: the red rival across the middle borders it too, and the green one is beside you to the east. In free-for-all games, a neutral Trade Post normally stands near the middle. In Twin Valleys, the mountain pushes posts out toward the colonies. The Wheel and Three Mesas are three-player maps, laid out like the other round planets: you in the south, red to the north-west, green to the north-east. On Long Haul 2v2 you land at the south end with red beside you to the east; green and purple land side by side at the far north end, green straight north of red and purple straight north of you. A Trade Post stands on the plaza at the very centre, as near to every colony.

Teams: 2 vs 2

With four colonies (You vs 3, or four in multiplayer, on Crossroads, Pinwheel, Four Isles, Twin Valleys or Long Haul 2v2), set Teams: 2 vs 2 on the new-game screen: the two western colonies play the two eastern ones. On your own, that makes the red colony to your north your computer ally against green and purple. Long Haul 2v2 is made for it (picking it turns Teams on): the two colonies at the south end play the two at the north end, red landing beside you, and the long middle between is where the war is fought.

  • Allies never shoot, shell, raid or capture each other, and their turrets and Flak leave each other alone. A computer ally can send spare Warbots to drive off raiders on your land if its own base is safe.
  • Neutral Trade Posts come in mirrored pairs where they fit. A shared post has a similar route from either team. Long Haul 2v2 keeps its shared central post.
  • A team game is won by Capture: take both enemy Command Bases and both colonies of your team win, even if one of you fell along the way.
  • In multiplayer the lobby shows the two teams, people first and then computer players. The host taps a person to move them to the other team (never more than two a side).

Rival doctrines

Every rival Command has spent decades reading the last order from home (Hold position. Further instructions to follow.) and each settled into a doctrine: how it plays. It's named in the briefing when you land and on its Command Base's panel. Pick one with Rival doctrine on the new-game screen (the Steward unless you choose), or Random; with three or four players, Each rival picks one for each computer colony, so you can mix them (in multiplayer the host picks each computer's in the lobby). Point at a doctrine to read in depth how it plays and where it's weak; on a touch screen tap it (while the rivals have doctrines of their own, a first tap only shows one, and a second gives it to them all). Now and then a rival speaks in its doctrine's voice, under its callsign: when it attacks you, when it fires a Missile at you, and when it surrenders (VERMILION (Warden): “Position held. Day 12,054.”).

Callsigns. The Accord named the colonies by their colours: COBALT (blue), VERMILION (red), VIRIDIAN (green) and TYRIAN (purple). The game names rivals that way, with the colour where it helps you find them on the map. Any callsign can run any doctrine.

Winning too easily? Tap the clock three times quickly (or press `) for a hidden menu that gives a rival a hand: a supply drop of goods, 4 armed Warbots or 3 Workers, delivered to its Command Base.

Every rival fortifies as the game goes on: more Pulse Turrets round its Command Base on the side facing you (up to three for a Steward, four for an Architect, two for a Salvager or Herald, six for a Warden), a Minigun Nest or Bunker by its fort nearest you every six minutes or so, and sometimes (about half the time for a Steward, less for a Salvager or Herald) an arc of Wall on the side of its base that faces you, with Gates on its roads. An Easy rival builds half as much. Bring Torches.

How a rival fights (Medium and up): it masses its army before it marches, on whichever of your forts takes the fewest to beat, and stays away a while from where it was just beaten. When your army comes near its base it calls its warbots home and keeps about a third of them there, a few in each Keep (a Salvager keeps its home guard thin). With a big lead in warbots it gathers outside gun range before storming your Command Base, the guns round it first. Even against a weakened army, heavy gun cover makes it gather a larger force or look for a cheaper fort to take. A Normal or Hard rival puts up a Mortar Pit to shell an army you park on its land out of reach of its guns. Its raids pull back when your warbots outnumber them. A Warden marches only in one big push, from about 22 minutes (sooner with a big lead); a Salvager's bands gather just outside your land before they strike; an Architect's Missiles go at what your colony lives on, and into an army at its gates.

Scout reports: now and then a message starting Scouts: tells you what a computer rival has just started: massing for a push on one of your forts (roughly how big), a band heading for one of your roads, a march on a fort, a storm of your Command Base (and when it goes in), a Warden's big push, or pulling back to hold its base while your warbots are at its gates; in a free-for-all, the rivals' marches and storms on each other too. Each comes once, a few seconds after it starts, with Go to the place, and stays in the message list. Scouts only tell what the map would show you. Scout reports in the pause menu turns them off.

Rival construction. Rivals can abandon sites that sit idle for several minutes while waiting for a road, freeing a build slot for work their Workers can reach.

Island crossings. A rival with no working Mines can use a lone Ore deposit. Rivals on Ore-poor islands can give their first Port and Ferry priority so they can land Warbots across the water.

The Army panel

Tap Army (Q) for the Army panel: how many warbots you have and where (at the Command Base, in garrisons, in the field, out of Ammo), and your orders. Pick a group size (2, 4, 8 or All; keys 1–4), then an order and tap the map: Attack (Q) a rival building, or a rival robot (they chase it down under a flag that follows it, then come back once it's destroyed), Hold ground (G) on open ground, or Station (V) them in an Outpost, Keep or Bunker of yours. Each tap sends another group until you tap Done (Army takes you back to the panel). A target too far from your garrisons to attack gets a rally flag beside it instead: they attack it, and once it falls they carry off what spilled out and come home. Below: every group in the field (Show, send more, Recall or Call back), every Howitzer by number and where it is, with Find (it takes you to it and picks it out with pulsing rings), Bombard and Drive, Recall all (C; on a touch screen tap it twice), and how many warbots every Outpost keeps. On a computer, Select all warbots (F) or Select all Howitzers to right-click orders. Your Howitzers are triangles on the minimap, and zoomed out a marker floats over each.

Marching: warbots you send to hold ground, station somewhere or call back march straight there without stopping to fight anything on the way (for up to a minute and a half), so you can pull them out of a losing fight. Once there, they fight as usual.

Rally point: tap an Outpost or Keep of yours and Make this the rally point for new warbots: every warbot the Bot Foundry makes from then on is stationed there instead of at the Command Base (a little flag flies over it), and comes back there after its orders. Ones that don't fit wait beside it. One at a time; tap it again to clear it.

Air (a second tab once you have an Airfield): each Airfield and its planes, with + Cargo Plane and + Vulcan Bomber to order more in its hangar; every Vulcan Bomber with Strike (or the big Strike: tap a target, E, for whichever is ready); every Cargo Plane with its air drops (Drop Outpost, 2 Warbots, 2 Workers); and every Airship, with what it's doing and Show (+ Airship orders one).

When the enemy destroys or captures a building of yours, a red warning stays on screen a few seconds longer than other messages (tap it to look).

Attack a building

Tap a rival building, choose how many warbots and tap Send warbots (or use the Army panel's Attack). They come from garrisons within 14 tiles of the target, and each garrison keeps its Keep at home (see below). Ordinary buildings are wrecked. Outposts become yours, along with their land. Capture the rival's Command Base to win.

Station warbots in a garrison

Every warbot belongs somewhere. All of them wait at your Command Base until you station some at an Outpost: tap the Outpost and use Station here (it brings that many, from the Command Base first), or set its Keep here (0, 1, 2, 5 or 10): the Command Base sends that many and replaces any that are lost. In Army, Every Outpost keeps 0 / 1 / 2 / 5 sets it for all of them at once. Wherever a warbot goes (a fight, a rally flag, an attack, repairs), it comes back to where it's stationed. Attacks and rally flags take warbots from the Command Base first, then from your Outposts, nearest first; stationed warbots come back to their Outpost when you call them back, and they always come out to defend it. When enemies come near on an Outpost's (or the Command Base's) panel: Go out and fight lets its warbots go out by themselves against enemies close by; Hold inside keeps them in until you press Send them out to fight (if it falls, they scramble out alive). Send them back to the Command Base on an Outpost releases its warbots. An Outpost holds 10 warbots and the Command Base 40. Lost track of some? Your robots (the robot count at the top) lists where every warbot is, and Show takes you to each.

Hold an area

Use the Army panel's Hold ground and tap open ground. Your warbots march there and plant a rally flag. On the way they keep to the roads in single file, each a step behind the one ahead, and spread out round the flag when they get there. They fight enemy bots that come near and attack enemy buildings within 3 tiles of the flag. Bots with a flat battery walk to the nearest garrison to recharge, then come back. In enemy land a warbot standing still runs its battery down after 15 seconds, so a flag there needs relieving now and then, and enemy raiders can't camp in your base for long. Tap the flag to send more or call them all home.

Recall all: the Army panel shows how many warbots are out in the field, with a Recall all button that ends every attack and rally flag at once, calls in warbots out picking up crates, and sends them all back to their garrisons, marching without stopping to fight (C on a computer; one already carrying a crate brings it in, and none goes after another crate for a minute). Handy when a raid hits home while your army is away.

Crates

Whenever a building is wrecked, blown up or dismantled, the goods inside it spill out as a pile of crates on its tile, with a count over it (one you dismantle leaves all its materials in the pile too), and a robot destroyed with a load drops it the same way. On your own land your Haulers collect the crates (a Freight Truck takes a big pile) and carry them to storage, as long as no enemy robot or gun is within 6 tiles: they wait until the fighting has moved on. Crates on claimed land stay until they're collected; on no-one's land they rot away after five minutes. Idle Haulers collect these crates before taking other jobs. Free Workers can help when no Hauler can take the load.

Loot

Wreck a rival building that has goods in it (a Depot above all) and the goods spill out as a pile of crates, with a count over it. Every warbot nearby picks up one crate, carries it back to your nearest Outpost or Command Base and goes back for another while any are left. Crates left at an Outpost are carried on to storage by Haulers, like a factory's output. Warbots carrying crates are easy prey: one shot down drops its crate where it falls, and any side's warbots can pick crates up, so the rival can win its goods back, and once your raiders leave, its Haulers collect whatever is still lying on its land.

Walls and Gates

In Paths, pick Walls (X) and drag a line. Every tile becomes a Wall (5), and where the line crosses a road or railway track a Gate goes instead (your haulers and trains run through it) (6 2). Tap a planned Wall to turn it into a Gate (a door on open ground for workers), and tap again to drop it. A finished Wall can become a Gate too: tap it and choose Make it a Gate (1 2 more). Unlike other buildings, Walls and Gates are paid straight from storage (what you have when you tap Build, the rest as it comes in), so they need no Hauler and no road, and they don't count toward the six-site limit. Free workers put them up in a few seconds each, standing beside each one on your side so nobody is left walled out; drag a double wall and the outer row goes up first.

A Wall stops every bot, yours included, and so do two Walls that only touch at a corner: a diagonal line of them is as tight as a straight one, with no squeezing between. Your bots drive straight through your own Gates (the barrier lifts for them), and a Gate on a road carries the road, so haulers never notice it. Enemy bots can't pass. Enemy warbots weigh it up: if there's a short way round they take it, and if not they stand at the wall and shoot their way through. A Wall takes 40 hits and a Gate 32 (they always pick the weakest spot, usually the Gate), every shot costs them Ammo, and a Missile does only 24, so it takes two to blow one open. When a Gate falls, the road under it is left standing.

Bunkers, Disruptors and Mortars (the Build menu's Defend tab): a Bunker (6 Cement, 2 Alloy; keys 4 U) is a concrete pillbox for 2 warbots. They shoot enemy robots within 3½ tiles from behind its walls with their own Ammo (it keeps a little to reload them) and recharge inside, and nothing can hit them until the Bunker falls. It claims no land and can go anywhere in your territory, even right beside an Outpost, a Gate or a bridgehead; shot down, it's wrecked and its warbots walk out. It's the answer when your Pulse Turrets run short of Crystal. A Disruptor Tower (4 Cement, 3 Alloy, 3 Crystal; keys 4 Z) sends out a pulse every 2½ seconds that drains 10 battery from every enemy robot within 4½ tiles and jams their guns for 2 seconds, so attackers fire far less while they're in reach, and once drained they slow down, hit at half power and have to go home to recharge. It kills nothing itself: pair it with a turret or a Bunker; it burns one Fuel for every 4 pulses, only while enemies are near. A Mortar Pit (5 Cement, 3 Alloy; keys 4 M) lobs a shell every 5 seconds at enemy robots 3 to 10 tiles away, aiming where they bunch up: 6 damage where it lands and 3 to the others within a tile and a half (up to 6 robots a shell). It can't hit anything closer than 3 tiles, so cover it with a Bunker or turret; one Ammo makes 3 shells. The rival builds all three too, beside whichever of its forts is under attack.

The Keep (14 Cement, 6 Alloy, 2 Chips; keys 4 K): a fortified concrete base with a heavy twin pulse cannon on its roof, firing Crystal out to 6 tiles, room for 25 warbots and gun slots for 6 of them, who shoot enemy robots within 4 tiles from cover with their own Ammo. It claims land like an Outpost, your roads run straight through it like an Outpost's, and it has to be captured, not knocked down. Within 7 tiles of it your Walls and Gates take a third less damage and mend themselves once left alone for 8 seconds, and your Pulse Turrets, Bunkers, Mortar Pits, Flak Guns and Disruptor Towers reach a tile further and fire a fifth faster (tap it, or start placing one, to see its ring). Keeps stand at least 16 tiles apart. Most rivals build one about a quarter of an hour in; a Warden builds its first early and up to three.

Bulwarks, Torches, Skirmishers, Arc Casters and Railgunners

Five specialists the Bot Foundry makes for your army alongside warbots. They garrison, rally, attack, reload, get repaired and are selected and ordered just like warbots (drag a box round them, the Army panel, control groups), and they count as warbots in the Army panel (its Select only row picks out one kind).

The Bulwark (three rounds of parts): a heavy, slow shield robot. A purple energy shield stands across its front (it glows at night and lights the ground before it) and stops fire coming from ahead: shots at the Bulwark, and at the six of your robots sheltering nearest it within about two tiles behind it (no more: select it and they show a faint violet ring), hit the shield instead, with a flash, a ripple running across the shield and sparks glancing off. The shield soaks up 40 damage and comes back 4 a second once it's had 2½ seconds without a hit; worn right down, it shatters and is out for 8 seconds. Shells, bombs, Missiles and fuel blasts come down over it, fire from the side or behind gets round it, a Torch's flame half wraps round it (and the rest burns the shield twice as fast), and a Disruptor Tower's pulse drains it. It turns, not quickly, to face the robot it's fighting (or the nearest armed enemy robot or gun): enemies coming at it from two sides get round it. Its twin blasters are weak (1 a shot): it leads, it doesn't fight alone. Its panel shows the shield's strength.

Formation. Send warbots and Torches with a Bulwark on an attack or to a rally flag. The Bulwark leads and the group keeps pace. In a fight, nearby warbots and Torches use the active shield if they can get behind it and still reach their target.

The Torch (two rounds of parts): a flamethrower robot. A short cone of fire, about two tiles long, burns every enemy robot in it (a little each, but all of them at once) and eats through buildings, and through defences, Walls and Gates fastest of all: one Torch breaks a Wall in about six seconds. It burns Fuel: a full tank is 12 seconds of fire, and an empty one sends it back to fill up from the Fuel kept at your Command Base, a Depot or a Fuel Station (one Fuel fills it), then back to what it was doing. A short orange bar under it shows a low tank. Fragile (18 health against a warbot's 12 and a Bulwark's 30), and destroyed with fuel aboard it explodes: keep it behind a Bulwark.

Scorch the earth (a Torch's panel, X, or the selection bar with Torches selected): tap a patch of fertile soil and the Torch walks over and sprays its whole tank into it, three seconds of fire, burning the soil for good so no Bio Farm grows crops there again. It needs a nearly full tank (90%, as after a refill) and goes back to refill afterwards. Several Torches take a patch each, round the one you tapped. Use it on a rival's farmland to cut its Fuel, or on your own before a rival takes it. A rival's raids may bring a Torch to your Bio Farms.

The Skirmisher (one round of parts): a fast, light raider on four big tyres, half again as quick as a warbot but with only 10 health. Sent at robots behind a Bulwark's shield, it runs round the shield and shoots from the side or the back, where its shots do double on the Bulwark; a pack splits both ways round it. It goes for Railgunners, Arc Casters, Howitzers (half again as much) and Workers before warbots, and every few seconds it dashes at twice its speed (a puff of dust and its tail thrusters flaring). It shoots Ammo like a warbot. Massed warbots and Minigun Nests cut it down.

The Arc Caster (two rounds of parts): a squat robot with a Tesla coil on its back. Every 1.7 seconds it throws a bolt of chain lightning (a jagged blue bolt that forks as it goes) at an enemy robot up to 2 tiles off; the bolt jumps on to the nearest enemy within 1.7 tiles of the last, up to three more times, a quarter weaker each jump: the more packed together they are, the more it hits. Lightning wears a Bulwark's shield down three times as fast as gunfire. Its capacitors hold 10 bolts and recharge with Crystal (one Crystal, five bolts) at your Command Base or a Depot, as a Torch fills with Fuel; a blue bar under it shows them running low. Thin (12 health) and short-ranged: Skirmishers that reach it tear it apart.

The Railgunner (three rounds of parts): a slow robot with a long open rail. It stops, charges (a star of light swells at the muzzle and the coil clamps brighten) and fires a slug up to 5 tiles in a bright streak at where its target stood when it began: it goes straight through every enemy robot in that line (10 to the first, 40% less to each after) and through a Bulwark's shield into the Bulwark, which it hits 1.7 times as hard. Then five seconds to reload. A quick robot (a dashing Skirmisher) can be out of the line by the time it fires, and closer than about a tile and a half it has only a weak sidearm. One Ammo is two slugs; it holds four.

How they fit: against a line of guns, a Bulwark in front means far fewer of your robots lost, and Torches then burn the guns and Walls down fast. A blob of warbots behind Bulwarks is broken by Torches (fire round the shields), Mortar Pits (shells over them; rivals build them toward a blob), Arc Casters (lightning through the pack and the shields) and Railgunners (slugs through the line); Skirmishers dive those two, and massed warbots beat Skirmishers. Each is about as strong as warbots of the same cost, stronger against what it counters and weaker against its own counter: mix them. Rivals build them by doctrine (a Salvager runs Skirmishers, a Warden Railgunners, an Architect a mix) and answer what you field.

The Howitzer is switched off for now: no one can build one, and a saved game loses any it had when it loads. When it's back, this is how it works. The Howitzer (Bot Foundry, three rounds of parts): mobile artillery for breaking defences. Tap it and choose Bombard a building (Q), then an enemy building: it drives to a firing spot within 13 tiles, digs in (about 7 seconds: spades down, barrel up), then fires one heavy shell every 5 seconds, over and over, until the target is rubble or its 12 shells are spent. Each shell knocks 4 off what it hits, 8 off a defence (turrets, Minigun Nests, Bunkers, Mortar Pits, Flak Guns, Disruptors, Walls, Gates, Outposts) and a little off buildings and robots round it: a Pulse Turret or Minigun Nest takes two shells, a Bunker four. Dug in it can't move: sent anywhere else, it packs up first (4 seconds). It outranges every defence but can't hit anything closer than 4 tiles, can't take a Command Base or Keep below a third of its armor (warbots still have to capture those), and is fragile: the rival sends warbots to kill any Howitzer shelling it, so keep an escort in front of it. Ammo: dug in on your own ground with a road within about 2½ tiles of it, a free hauler brings it Ammo from your nearest storage (4 shells an Ammo), so plan where your guns sit; otherwise it packs up, reloads 3 Ammo at a garrison or storage and comes back. Hurt (below 60%), it drives to a Repair Station within 30 tiles, gets mended inside, then goes back to its target or its post. A new Howitzer rolls off the Foundry and waits beside it; it stays wherever you send it (its post), and on its panel you choose Fire at will (it digs in and shells any enemy building in reach, without moving) or Hold fire (only on your orders). With a mouse, select it and right-click a rival building to bombard it, or ground to move it.

The Minigun Nest (2 Cement, 1 Alloy; keys 4 G): a cheap, short-range deterrent. A six-barrel rotary gun on an armoured mount spins up and hoses the nearest enemy robot within 3½ tiles with a stream of tracer rounds, twelve a second, each doing little (it shreds workers and haulers, but takes a while on a warbot), and every robot it hits is pinned: it moves at half speed and shoots slower for a moment, so your other guns get longer at it. It fires Ammo, 60 rounds each. Two of them cost about the same as a Pulse Turret and do about as well against a small raid.

Walls earn their keep across a narrow pass, with Pulse Turrets just behind: the attackers stand still in turret range while they work. Repair a battered Wall from its panel like any building. On Chokepoint maps the rival walls off the mouths of the channels on its side on Normal and Hard, and puts back any piece you break.

Missiles

A Missile Factory builds each Missile from 2 Alloy, 1 Chip and 1 Fuel, taking 20 seconds, and haulers load them into a Missile Silo (it holds two and reloads in a full 60 seconds). To fire, tap your Silo and press Aim missile: the map zooms out and its 22-tile range shows as a red ring on the ground. Tap any spot inside it (a rival building, any patch of ground, or a rival robot or vehicle, hit where it stood when fired: it can move out of the way) to see the blast radius and flight path, then tap Fire (or, with a mouse, click the same spot again). On a touch screen the map stays close enough to aim with a finger: tap to drop the crosshair and drag it to adjust. You can also tap Army, then a rival building, then Fire missile. A hit does 24 damage, which wrecks most buildings outright (turrets, factories, even another Silo), and the blast does 5 damage to other rival buildings and 9 to bots within about 2 tiles. Missiles knock out Outposts and Bunkers too (an Outpost goes in one hit, a Bunker in two; its land goes with it and its warbots spill out). Only the Command Base and Keeps can't be knocked below about a third of their armor, so send warbots right behind the missile to capture them. A fort under a quarter of its armor sends every armed warbot inside out to fight, and taking one sends anyone still inside running, unharmed. Missiles are expensive, so save them for turret lines, Outposts and the Command Base. When the rival fires at you, a flashing red ring shows where it will land. The rival saves up for missiles too once its colony is going, and aims them at your turrets and Outposts first. A Silo shows its missile only when it's loaded and ready to fire (a Nuclear Silo once its bomb is armed): an empty launch pit means it's reloading or waiting for Missiles.

Ammo

Every warbot carries 10 shots. Against a building it fires from up to about 2½ tiles away, each shot knocking 2 off it (1 off a Wall or Gate: they're built to hold), so a big group can all fire at once. Each hit uses one shot, and a warbot reloads inside your Command Base or an Outpost, 5 shots per Ammo. Haulers keep each garrison stocked (Command Base 10, Outposts 6) from an Ammo Plant. A warbot with no ammo can't fight at all: it drops out of the fight and heads back to a garrison to reload (one holding a rally flag reloads and returns), and only armed warbots answer attack orders, rally flags and garrison sorties. A small brass bar under a warbot means it's running low.

Repairs

Nothing heals by itself: a damaged bot stays damaged and a battered building stays battered until you fix it. A Repair Station works like a Fuel Station for health. It fixes three bots at a time using Alloy, which haulers keep stocked (up to 3, and an empty station gets the first Alloy that reaches storage): one Alloy repairs 12 health, enough for a whole Warbot or two workers. Workers below 60% health drop in on their own when they have nothing to do, and warbots below 70% go once their garrison has been quiet for a few seconds, leaving at least one behind on guard. An idle warbot (in a garrison or standing about) with any damage at all goes too when no enemy robot or gun is within 9 tiles and a Repair Station with Alloy stands within 30. A warbot badly hurt in a fight (below 35%) pulls out for repairs on its own when a Repair Station can fix it, and goes back to its attack or rally flag once it's mended. In Colony free play it fixes robots that creatures have mauled, too.

To fix a building, tap it and press Repair. You pay the Cement up front, 1 for every 6 missing armor (a badly hit Command Base might cost 4), and a free worker walks over and restores about 2 armor a second. The button shows the price and turns grey if you can't afford it. If one of your buildings has been damaged and left alone for a while, a message reminds you.

Raids

Warbots holding a rally flag in enemy land, with nothing there to fight, tear up the enemy's roads near the flag (but not bridges or tunnels, which only a Missile can crater), junctions first: one tile about every four seconds with three warbots. A cut highway strands the haulers beyond it until the road is re-laid, so a raid on the right stretch can starve a whole front.

The rival raids you too. Every few minutes (rarely on Easy, often on Hard, all the time if it's a Salvager), once it has warbots to spare, it sends a small band to your busiest road away from your turrets and garrisons. They shoot up passing Haulers and Trucks, cut the road, then hit whatever stands nearby before heading home. You get a warning with the spot. Drive them off with an attack or a rally flag, re-lay the road, and protect long highways with a turret or two and warbots in the forward Outposts. A second route for the important runs keeps goods moving while you repair. Missiles also crater the roads where they land.

Ordering bots

The Bot Foundry only builds the bots you order. Tap it, then press +1 or +5 next to Workers or Warbots; Clear cancels that queue. With nothing queued it sits idle and leaves your Alloy and Chips in storage.

Build order. The Foundry works down its list of bot types from the top. Use the ▲ ▼ arrows beside each type to move it up or down, and pick how mixed orders are built: Take turns builds one of each type you've ordered in turn, in the list's order; Top of the list first builds every bot of the top type you've ordered before moving on to the next. The type it's building now is highlighted. Each Foundry keeps its own order, and it's saved with the game.

War scars

A long war scars the land. Fight over the same ground for an hour and the green between two colonies becomes a World War One battlefield: shell holes overlapping into churned mud, big craters that fill with dark puddles when it rains, grass burned to ash, charred stumps where the woods stood, robot graveyards and the bleached bones of the animals the blasts caught. None of it slows a robot.

  • Craters from every real explosion, sized by the weapon: a robot going up or a Mortar shell leaves a shell hole; a Howitzer's shell, a bomb, a Missile, a fuel blast or a crash a big crater; a nuclear blast a field of craters on black glass.
  • Scorch and ash: blasts, Torches and napalm burn the grass. A light scorch grows back in about ten minutes; the heart of a blast and napalm leave ash for good, and trees on ash, in a big crater or on ground shelled twice are charred stumps. A Grove Planter greens a scarred patch again where its Agribot plants a grove.
  • Scrap stays where each robot fell (bits from a Worker, a broken hull from a warbot, a burnt-out wreck from a Bulwark, truck, train or Crawler), joining into graveyards where the fighting goes on; buildings destroyed by force leave their rubble, and shot-down aircraft their wrecks, for good.
  • Salvage: idle Haulers cut up scrap on your land within 2½ tiles of a road they drive (5 seconds) and carry the whole Alloy in it to storage, a load at a time: a warbot's scrap is half an Alloy, a heavy wreck 1½ to 3. Run a road out to a graveyard to clear it. The rivals salvage too.
  • The Murmur drain the charge left in wreckage: their tunnels lean toward big heaps of scrap, and on Active an exit by one brings an extra Scuttler.
  • The land heals: where nobody has fought for a few minutes, the scorch and ash fade and grass and small flowers come up over the next eight minutes or so, on the old craters too. Craters, stumps, scrap and graveyards stay.
  • Burning oil: an Oil Pump destroyed by an enemy burns as a roaring jet of flame under thick black smoke for three to four minutes, lighting the night, then smoulders as a blackened ruin; a destroyed Refinery or Fuel Tank burns lower and shorter. Only for looks: you can build there again at once.
  • Smoke, fire and ash: burning wrecks and big blasts send up tall smoke that leans with the wind and lingers, fires creep a little over scorched grass and light the ground at night, ash and embers drift over the fighting, and big blasts (a Missile, Ringfall, fuel, a nuclear blast, the Lance) send a shockwave along the ground. Only for looks.
  • The setting (pause menu → Settings → War scars, kept on this device): Full (default) as above; Light: only big blasts leave lasting craters and the scorch always grows back; Off: only marks that fade. Each game keeps the setting it started with (in multiplayer, the host's).

Pulse Turrets near your Command Base and Outposts wear down raids, as long as they get Crystal. Missiles are the best way to deal with a rival turret line before your warbots walk in.

The Murmur & research

Every game · research: Colony

The Murmur

The planet's native life lives underground. The robots heard it before they ever saw it, a low sound in the ground, and logged it as the Murmur. They're nobody's side: they raid every colony alike, and never count for victory. Set The Murmur to Off, Quiet or Active (the default) on the new-game screen. Their nests sit out in the wilds (violet on the minimap), away from the bases, by Crystal and rich deposits; in Versus they're placed fairly, the same for every colony. None live on Argos, the moon: there are no nests and no raids there.

By night they tunnel. Every night each nest's tunnels spread further, branching toward power (Generators, Fuel Stations, storage full of Crystal or Fuel), more each night: raised ridges of heaved earth with violet veins in them, faint by day, glowing at night with pulses running back to the nest. They pass under roads, buildings and Walls. New tunnel exits open along them (violet rings on the minimap), and a minute after nightfall packs come up through them to raid whatever's near: the more exits, the bigger the raids. About 40 seconds before nightfall a message says the Murmur are stirring. At dawn the ones that came up go back down. Days are for striking the nests; nights for holding the line. By day the nests send nothing out, in Colony as in Versus. Always night (Time of day) only changes the look: they still come out at the game clock's nightfall, after the warning.

How they hunt (on Active; on Quiet they come as plain packs). At dusk one of them surfaces at the edge of your land and stands still, facing the colony, then is gone: where it stood is where the night's strike falls. They pick what your guns don't cover (a lone Harvester, a far Oil Pump, an unguarded Fuel Station), gather out of sight beyond your land and your guns' reach, and come in together, Brutes in front taking the fire and Scuttlers from several sides. A few go first for what lights and charges the colony near the target: a Fuel Station or Generator they reach goes dark until dawn, and the street lights round it go out one by one. Scuttlers scatter when warbots close in, and come back; a pack that loses half its number goes back down and may come again later that night. They take their dead with them. They remember where they were killed and keep their exits away from it, and come back where they fed well. After a couple of nights, with warbots in your colony, a small pack may strike one side first to draw them off before the main pack hits the other. Against them: a gun by each Fuel Station and Generator, warbots near where the watcher stood, some warbots kept back when the first pack shows.

ScuttlerQuick and small (6 health). Runs in packs and bites any bot caught in the open: Haulers, Workers, Trucks.
LeechActive only, from the second night. Latches onto a Fuel Station, Generator or streetlamp and drinks: the lights go out, the station charges nothing, robots charging there run down, and full, it takes the charge home to grow its nest. Guard the power with guns; once it's dead, tap the dark building → Repair to light it again.
SpitterActive only, from the second night. Stops a few tiles off a gun or Wall behind the Brutes and spits at it: armor off, and a gun it hits shorts out for a few seconds. Send warbots at it; keep guns covering each other.
SapperActive only, from the third night. Digs in outside your Walls and guns, goes under them (nothing reaches it there; you see the ground heave) and comes up inside. Meet it with warbots where it comes up. Its tunnel stays, so exits can open inside your Walls later.
BruteBig and armoured (24 health), in the packs from the second night (the fourth on Quiet). Tears up roads and batters buildings, and breaks through Walls and Gates (slowly: about 40 seconds for one Brute on a Wall). The Murmur never wreck any other finished building, but they can wreck a construction site.

On Active the Murmur gather before striking. A blocked approach makes them regroup elsewhere or withdraw. They favor cuts that interrupt useful power, such as a busy Fuel Station with no backup. As the main pack waits, a small raid may draw defenders away from its real target. Keep some warbots at the approach the dusk watcher marked.

Nests grow a level every ten minutes (up to 3), with more guards. Pulse Turrets (keep them in Crystal) shoot the Murmur on sight, and garrisons send their warbots out at packs that come close or batter the buildings round them. Collapse a tunnel exit with warbots (Army → Attack, then tap it: by day four are plenty): it stays shut for 15 minutes. To clear a nest, put an Outpost within reach, fill it with armed warbots and tap the nest to attack (or hold a rally flag beside it). Its guards fight back; once it falls, the goods it hoarded spill out for your warbots to carry home, its creatures scatter, its whole tunnel network dies back and its exits close, and the land around it is free to build on (nothing can be built within 2 tiles of a nest). The rival colonies live with them too: they collapse exits near their land and strike nests by day, first one sitting on Ore or oil they're running short of.

Colony goals

Colony free play has an optional ladder of Colony goals that leads you into the deeper systems, in four tiers: Groundwork (a traffic light or overpass, a pipeline between two buildings, a Generator, a Depot with supply orders, a research upgrade), Long hauls (30 tiles of asphalt, a Road Train, a Freight Train, a run on a Trade Terminal's route, a Murmur nest cleared), Across the planet (a Ropeway, a Viaduct, a Port with a Container Ship, a Cargo Plane delivery, goods flown along an Airship line) and Accord scale (goods moved by Maglev pod, three Freight Trains at once, 100 Alloy a minute, 100 robots, every upgrade). Goals the planet can't offer, such as a Port with no sea, are left out. The story

Ithaca I

The planet is Ithaca I, a coral-red world with a ring. Four robot vanguards were sent here to make it ready for colonists, and every game is one more landing, a terrafall: a Command Base dropped from orbit and run by a Command intelligence. That's you. The story stays in the margins; nothing in it changes how the game plays.

An armed enemy warbot within 3 tiles jams it; jammed or without power its clock runs back at half speed. Destroyed, the transmission is lost. Transmitting draws the Murmur.
  • Rivals build them late, one weapon and two megastructures at most (a Dome put up in answer to a threat doesn't count): an Architect or Steward builds Arrival City from about 15 minutes in (in Domination, every rival), and they march on yours to take it; a Warden builds the Orbital Lift from about 18 minutes, a Herald once a game runs past about 22, calling down Security when attacked or before a push; a Salvager builds none, but sends a band to wait at your anchor when a climber comes down. In a Nuclear race one picks the Lance or the bomb (an Architect leans to the Lance, a Herald to the nukes; a strung-out colony or a Shield Dome of yours tips it to the Lance), and one threatened from afar puts up a Dome. With the Great Uplink win on, a Steward builds the Great Uplink instead of Arrival City and transmits once its Generators are fed and its army is home; every rival makes a foe's transmission its first target, with warbots to jam it and Missiles, bombers and the Lance.

Nuclear race

Optional

With Nuclear race on (the new-game screen; on by default) a few deposits of glowing green Uranium are scattered across the planet. There aren't many, so whoever holds them wins the race.

  • Dig it: an Ore Mine within 4 tiles of a deposit digs its Uranium before any ore.
  • Enrich it: a Nuclear Reactor turns 2 Uranium into 1 Enriched Uranium. Guard it: destroyed by a rival, its core is breached (see Radiation below).
  • Build the bomb: a Nuclear Silo assembles one nuclear missile at a time from 6 Enriched Uranium, 4 Alloy and 2 Chips, a minute's work once the parts are in. That's 12 Uranium a bomb.

Tap the Silo and press Aim nuclear missile: its 40-tile range shows on the ground. Tap a spot to see the 6-tile blast, then Launch. The missile takes 30 seconds to land, and the target is warned the moment it launches, with the blast ring and a countdown on their map. A Nuclear missile incoming card appears with an Evacuate button: press it and every robot in the ring, garrisons and the workers inside buildings included, runs out to safe ground and waits; nothing is sent back in until the blast, then everyone goes back to work. The rival evacuates too. Everything inside the ring is wiped out: buildings, robots and roads, yours included. A Command Base caught in the blast survives with its garrison gone and almost no armor, ready for warbots to capture, but a missile that lands on a Command Base destroys it: that colony is out, and what's left of it is yours. The rival aims for your Command Base when it's in range, so keep your Silos' targets in mind and theirs out of reach. The ground stays scorched for 4 minutes, and nothing can be built or laid there until it cools. The rival races for the bomb too, and fires as soon as it has one.

Radiation

A nuke's fallout and a breached Reactor leave a radiation zone: a sickly green-yellow haze over dead grey grass, its edge marked by a dashed yellow ring (and on the minimap). It shrinks as it cools and is gone on its own: a nuke's starts 7 tiles round where it landed and lasts 5½ minutes; a Reactor's starts 4 tiles round it and lasts 5 minutes. A nuke a Shield Dome stops leaves none. A Reactor only breaches when a rival destroys it: dismantled or moved, it shuts down safely.

  • Robots out in a zone lose 2% of their health a second at its middle (half that at its edge): walking straight through costs a little, standing in it kills in about a minute. Inside buildings and in the air they're safe. They glow green while it hurts them, and a faint Geiger crackle plays when a selected robot (or the camera) is in one.
  • Buildings in a zone work at half speed, and nothing new can be built in one (the placement message says how long until it clears).
  • Workers and Haulers go round zones where they can and won't gather or pave in one; jobs you leave inside they still go to. The rival keeps its sites out too and marches round them.
  • A breached Reactor is a smoking ruin with a broken stack; at night it sends a green and blue column of light into the sky for about 5 minutes before it goes cold.

Missions

The tutorial

Tap Missions on the main menu. The missions are the tutorial campaign: hand-picked planets, each with its own goals, starting conditions and features, that teach the game one idea at a time, from your very first building to all-out war. They come in seven chapters, each building on the last: the colony and its logistics first, in peace, then creatures and rivals. From the second mission on you land with the colony the earlier missions taught already built and joined up, so you get straight to the new idea. Play them in order (the first one you haven't finished is marked Next up) or pick any: every mission is open from the start.

Pick Easy, Normal or Hard: harder levels raise the goals, start you with fewer goods and bring a tougher rival, and Hard puts most missions on the clock. The briefing card sets the scene; the goal banner marks the next goal with a hint, lights up the button it needs (or rings the building to tap) and shows any time left. Once a building it asks for is going up, the hint says to wait for it (or to lay its road) rather than build another.

Taking the last rival Command Base ends the game, so in a mission it waits for the rest of your goals: until they are done it holds at 1 armor, and its panel says Finish your other goals first.

1. First Steps

One idea at a time, in peace.

  1. TouchdownColony. Place a Harvester by the stalk groves, join it to your roads and watch the Fiber come in.
  2. GroundworkColony. Draw your first road in Paths out to the boulders, quarry Stone and bake it into Cement.
  3. RechargeColony. Robots run on batteries: build a Fuel Station, then dig Ore and tap Crystal.
  4. Hot MetalColony. Move a Cement Works beside its Quarry, then smelt your first Alloy beside an Ore Mine: conveyors feed them.

2. Growing the Colony

Bots that build bots, Fuel from farms, groves that regrow, and Outposts that push the border out.

  1. Assembly LineColony. Etch Chips, build a Bot Foundry, order new robots and raise a stock limit.
  2. Green FuelColony. The landing burned your Fuel: grow it at a Bio Farm with Agribots, and plant groves that regrow.
  3. FrontierColony. Dismantle a spent mine, push your border out with Outposts and mine the rich frontier.

3. Roads and Traffic

Keep the Haulers moving.

  1. Rush HourColony. Join a far Depot with Route to, add Haulers, then sort the traffic with one-way lanes and lights.
  2. CrossingsColony. Bridge a pond, tunnel through a crag and cut a Ramp up a ledge.
  3. BlacktopColony. Pump oil, cook Asphalt and send your Pavers out to lay new road and surface your roads.
  4. ArterialColony. Lay a pair of Express lanes, link both ramps and send Freight Truck deliveries to the survey Depot.

4. Industry and Trade

Pipelines and power, research, far Depots and trucks, railways, trade and planet events.

  1. Pipe DreamsColony. Refine Crude into Fuel, pipe it, and speed your factories up with a Generator.
  2. BreakthroughColony. Build a Research Lab and research upgrades for the whole colony.
  3. Long HaulColony. A forward Depot up a long valley, a supply order to keep it stocked, and Freight Trucks.
  4. Iron RoadColony. Lay track to a far Crystal field and run a Freight Train between two stations.
  5. Phase 2Colony. Savanna: the Crystal lies across a river; build a Maglev Terminal, run the line over the river, power both ends, move Crystal home by pod and chain a second line on from a hub.
  6. The ScheduleColony. A survey camp at the end of a long road to the north: build a Mass Driver by your base and a Catcher at the camp, aim it, power it with a Generator and throw the camp its Cement.
  7. Trade WindsColony. Dock a Trade Terminal at a Trade Post, run a trade route by Road Train and ride out planet events.

5. Sea and Sky

Ports and ships, Viaducts and Ropeways, a Crawler and an airlift, and an Airship line.

  1. HarbourColony. Islands: build a Port and ships, found a second Port across the sea and ship it goods.
  2. Sky BridgesColony. Sky islands: span the canyon with a Viaduct and a Ropeway to reach the Crystal beyond.
  3. AirliftColony. Plateaus: drive a Crawler off the plateau, drop an Outpost by parachute and supply by air.
  4. Mooring LinesColony. Sky islands: raise a Mooring Mast at home and one by your Drop Outpost across the canyon, link them, and fly Crystal home by Airship.

6. First Contact

The Murmur's nests, their cavern lair, how they hunt and their other forms, a rival to practise on, a border war and a chokepoint to hold.

  1. The MurmurColony with the Murmur. Turrets and warbots ready by nightfall, when the packs come up: collapse tunnel exits, clear their nests.
  2. Into the HollowColony. The Hollow: land under a breach, cut Fiber from the Bloom veins, run a Ropeway to the floating Crystal and repair what the falling rock hits.
  3. What Comes UpColony with the Murmur Active. Repair a Fuel Station a Leech drank dry, guard the power with a Pulse Turret, meet the strike where the watcher stood, and kill a Leech, a Spitter and a Sapper.
  4. Live FireVersus. Your first rival, a quiet one: recycle your spare Workers, command warbots, take its Outposts, then its Command Base.
  5. Border DisputeVersus. Take the rival’s Outposts, carry off loot and hold a share of the land.
  6. Hold the LineVersus. Chokepoint: a Herald (rusher) attacks early. Wall the channels, man a Bunker and hold out.

7. War

Ammo and repairs, trade convoys, siege robots and missiles, the nuclear race and a final assault.

  1. Supply LinesVersus. Keep the Ammo flowing, mend the wounded, set a rally point and take the rival base.
  2. ConvoyVersus. A Salvager (raider) raids every road: run a trade route with Escort on, wreck one of its convoys and carry the loot home.
  3. Siege BreakersVersus. A Warden (turtle) behind Walls, turrets and a Bulwark picket: break its shields with Arc Casters and Skirmishers, burn in with Torches and missiles, and burn its farmland.
  4. Chain ReactionVersus. A Nuclear race: enrich Uranium, build a Nuclear Silo and launch.
  5. Aimed the Other WayVersus. The megastructures: put up a Shield Dome by a Generator, build the Lance of Corvane, and fire it down a row of rival depots.
  6. King of the HillVersus for three. The Wheel: hold two of the three Uplinks, longer than two rivals of random doctrine.
  7. Final DescentVersus. Canyon maze and a seasoned rival: a Keep, Flak Guns, Vulcan Bombers and the final capture.

Multiplayer

2 to 4 people

Play one game with friends, each on your own phone, tablet or computer (any mix), with computer rivals in any places left over. Everyone sees the whole map.

  • Host: main menu, Multiplayer, type your name and tap Host a game. The lobby shows a five-character room code; Share sends it with a link that opens the game straight to it.
  • Join: Multiplayer, your name, the code, Join (or just open the link).
  • Set up: the host taps Change settings (the usual new-game screen: map type, biome, size, rules, how many colonies in all and how hard the computer rivals are), picks each computer's doctrine from its row in the lobby, then Start game. Four people need a map with room for four (Crossroads, Pinwheel, Four Isles or Twin Valleys); with four colonies the host can also set Teams: 2 vs 2 and move people between the teams in the lobby.

You can rejoin a paused match without advancing the game. Cancel keeps your seat available to retry.

Co-op: one colony, two players. In Custom Game pick Versus or Colony and tap Host a co-op game: whoever joins your room shares your colony (one economy, one army, one win or loss; in the lobby the host taps a player to move them into or out of their colony, and Colony mode in multiplayer is always co-op). Either of you can do anything, each with your own view and menus. Your partner's cursor shows as a coloured dot with their name; . (or Ping on the bar) marks a spot on both maps and minimaps with a sound; sites carry a badge with the initial of whoever placed them, and the message log says who did what. A role in the lobby (Both, Economy, Military) only picks which alerts pop up for you. Dismantling something your partner placed in the last minute asks first. If one of you leaves, the other keeps the colony.

In the game, the menu button opens the menu without stopping the game; Pause the game for everyone stops all of you, and anyone can resume. The game runs at one speed for everyone. If someone's connection drops the others wait for them, and after 12 seconds the computer runs their colony; when they're back it catches up and hands it back. A page that reloads (or a closed app) shows Rejoin multiplayer game on the main menu. Leave the game hands your colony to the computer. Multiplayer games aren't saved for Continue.

Statistics

The end-of-game report. When a game ends, the end screen tells the story of it: the moments that mattered (your first robot, the first attack each way, buildings lost, when a good ran out and for how long, the deciding battle or capture), where your goods were lost (buildings destroyed, robots destroyed with their loads, crates left lying) and one Next time, try tip drawn from what happened.

Open Statistics from the pause menu at any time, or from the end-of-game screen. You get totals, then graphs over time of your bots, the land you hold, any good you pick, your economy's size (goods made a minute, buildings, or workers and vehicles) and your military's size (army or defences), with the rival's line beside yours in Versus. Drag a finger across a graph to read exact values.

How busy your buildings were ranks every kind of building by the share of the time it spent working. A busy one (near 100%) is a bottleneck worth doubling; a quiet one is waiting on inputs, workers or pickups. Tap a bar to see that building type's busy time over the game. Each building's own panel also shows how busy it has been in the last few minutes.

Goods panel

Tap any good's counter in the top bar to open the Goods panel. It shows how much you have (in storage and waiting for pickup at buildings), how much was made and used per minute lately and the net rate, how much buildings want right now, and which buildings make and use it, with how many are working. A red shortfall warning means buildings are waiting on a good you have none of. Switch goods with the buttons at the top.

Why idle? A factory that isn't making anything (the Bot Foundry, the Smelter or any other) says why in its own panel. Why idle? follows the missing input back down the supply chain and stops at the real cause, in plain words: No Crystal: your Crystal Tap ran dry 3 minutes ago and no new one is being built. Show takes you to the building at fault; Build places the missing one. Disconnected road carriers and unassigned deliveries have their own messages. When the colony has gone without a good for a while with something waiting for it, that good's counter in the top bar gets a red ring, and the Goods panel opens with what's holding the colony up.

Saving and settings

  • Rival difficulty. In the pause menu of a Custom Game with computer rivals, pick Easy, Medium, Normal or Hard for all computer colonies. In multiplayer the host can change it on a shared turn. Missions keep their selected difficulty.
  • Solo games keep up to five restore points, one per in-game minute. In the pause menu or on the game-over screen, pick a point by its age, select Restore and confirm. The main menu shows the latest solo history after a reload. A browser with little storage keeps fewer points. Multiplayer has no restore points. Continue uses the latest resume save, also updated when you leave. If the browser refuses to update it when you load a save, choose Save → File in the pause menu to keep a copy. Mission progress is kept separately on each device.
  • Save slots. Besides the autosave there are five slots on the device: Slots under Save in the pause menu opens Saved games, listing the Autosave and Slot 1–5 with each one's map, mode, game time and the date it was saved. Name a slot if you like and tap Save here; Load and Delete do what they say (both ask first where it matters). The main menu has Saved games too, and its Continue card shows the game it will resume: the newest save of them all.
  • A new game opens with a short briefing card (a line from your colony's log, where you landed, your goal, notes on the map) while the game waits paused. It closes by itself after a few seconds; tap anywhere to start sooner.
  • The speed button opens 0.75×, 0.875×, 1×, 2× and 3×. The pause menu has Resume, Settings, Statistics, Photo mode, a short How to play, four ways to Save (This planet, Slots: the save slots, File: a save file to download, and Cloud), Feedback and Save & quit. Settings has Music, Effects and Ambience volume, Graphics, switches for Full screen, Weather, Scout reports and Vibration, and Time of day (Cycle, Day or Night; no Day with the Murmur in the game); Back returns.
  • Saves stay on the device you play on (in that browser). To carry a game to another device, open the pause menu and tap File under Save (Download save file), then on the other device tap Load a save file on the main menu and pick it: the game carries on where you left it.
  • Cloud saves: no file to move? Tap Cloud under Save in the pause menu (Save to the cloud). You get a code (like K7PQ4-XMR2B), a link and a QR code of the link, with Copy link (and Share on a phone). On the other device, point its camera at the QR code, open the link, or tap Load from a code on the main menu and type the code: the game loads there, and the save that device had is kept as a backup. Saving the same game to the cloud again keeps its code up to date; New code swaps it for a fresh one (the old one stops working). A code lasts 30 days after the last save to it. There's no account: the code is the only key, so share it only with people you'd hand the game to. Multiplayer games aren't saved to the cloud. On an iPhone, a game added to the Home Screen keeps its saves apart from Safari: open it from the Home Screen and type the code there.
  • Send feedback: found a bug, have an idea, or think something is too strong or too weak? Tap the small Feedback button beside Lore at the foot of the main menu, or Feedback in the pause menu. Pick Bug, Idea, Balance or Other, write what happened (up to 5,000 characters) and, if you'd like a reply, an email or name. It goes to the game's maker with the game's version and your device and screen size and, from a game, its map, seed, time and last few messages; nothing else. In a game, Attach my current game (ticked for a Bug) sends its save file too, so the bug can be played back exactly, and Attach a picture of the map (ticked for a Bug too) sends a picture of the map as it is on screen. If it can't be sent (no connection, or too many reports at once) the form says why and keeps what you wrote. Opening it changes nothing in the game: a paused game stays paused. Esc or Back closes it.
  • Day and night: the planet turns, 12 minutes of game time round: a golden morning, day, an orange evening as the lights come on, and night, when buildings glow, streetlights come on along your roads (warm orange over concrete, brighter white over asphalt) and vehicles drive with headlights. Sunsets and sunrises light the land from the sun's side with long shadows, and every region has its own: orange and pink over the Shoals' glittering sea, amber and red with glowing dust in the Rust and the Long Grass, teal and violet in the Bloom as its glow wakes, pastel pink over the Polar Caps, an ember sky in the Cinderlands, a hard terminator on Argos (on Graphics Saver, just their colours). After dark a whole colony glows: a haze of light in its colour over its buildings, its roads traced with glowing edge lines and its border lit. It's only for looks. The clock shows a sun or a moon. Time of day (pause menu, or Custom Game on the main menu) switches between Cycle, Always day and Always night; with the Murmur in the game there's no Always day, since they live by the night (Always night is only a look: they still come out at the game clock's nightfall). Each colony's streetlights carry a light strip in its colour, and at night Flak Guns sweep the sky with searchlights that lock onto enemy aircraft.
  • Graphics (pause menu, or Custom Game's Rules on the main menu; kept on this device, and it changes at once): Auto, the default, draws as sharp as the screen allows, up to 2×, and eases off if frames come slowly. Saver is for older phones and long sessions: 1×, 30 frames a second, fewer effects and plainer night lights, without the swaying plants, frost, aurora and far-zoom tilt. High draws a big desktop screen at its full 2× with sharper ground up close and more smoke and sparks, and Ultra adds the screen's full sharpness, the densest effects and weather, and robots carrying lights at night. Installed as an app, the game looks just as it does in a browser tab.
  • Weather: now and then a storm blows in for a minute or two, then clears: a sandstorm on the red desert, a rainstorm with fog banks and lightning in the forest, a blizzard on the poles, ash and embers falling on the ashlands, ring dust streaking over the Moon, a gale stripping the autumn leaves, dust devils on the savanna, an aurora over the alien bloom, and a thick fog on the swamp (with fireflies at night). It's only for looks (it changes nothing in the game). Turn it off with Weather in the pause menu or Custom Game on the main menu.
  • Wildlife: Watch deer and foxes in the woods, wildebeest and hares on the savanna, and broad-snouted alligators and turtles in Blackwater. The Bloom has armored hinge-backs, twin-tailed switchtails and rib-winged sailwings, all with amber eyes. Red crabs sidestep along Shoals beaches, gulls bank on black-tipped wings, and dolphins arc through the air over open water. Hares in the dry regions have sandy coats. The hares on the Polar Caps are white. Look for bats banking on scalloped wings near rocky ground in the Rust at dusk. Hawks have broad, rusty tails, vultures rock on fingered wings, and crows and ravens can be told apart by their fan and wedge tails. The Hollow has blind crawlers and pale cave sailwings with ragged wings. Look for white-tipped red fox tails, rounded Arctic fox ears, white-cheeked geese, and domed turtle shells. Blackwater herons strike at fish with a quick sweep of the neck. Woodland and snowy owls turn their heads at night, and rock lizards dart with a long swinging tail. In Cinderlands, ash beetles have warm shell seams and ember moths have bands on their wings. Wildlife scatters at gunfire. Roads, track and buildings also shrink its habitat, so busy colonies grow quieter even between fights. Argos is airless. In Active free play, working industry can feed larger Murmur packs after the first night.
  • Night lighting. Night lights follow each structure’s footprint and switch off when it is lost. Express gantries light their two lanes independently.
  • Wind, water and sky. One wind blows across the planet, the same way the clouds drift: up close the trees sway with it, and explosions shove the plants outward. The sun sparkles on the water by day and small waves lap the shores; at night the Ring casts a faint silver light (different on every planet), the Ring and Argos shine back from the water with stars on clear nights, and an aurora drifts over the Polar Caps, Argos and the Bloom. After rain the ground stays wet for a while and mirrors the lights at night; on the cold maps frost creeps in at dawn and melts by midday. Zoomed right out the camera tilts a little and the land carries on past the map's edge into the haze. The main menu shows Ithaca I going slowly round Corvane, with the Ring, Argos, the Carriers and the war flickering on its night side. Graphics Saver leaves out the swaying, frost, aurora and tilt; reduce motion on your device and nothing sways or drifts. Only for looks.
  • Clouds, remains and beacons. The Command Base casts a short beam from evening to dawn. By day (with Weather on) cloud shadows drift over the land with the wind, darker in a spell of weather (none on Argos, in the Hollow or on Graphics Saver). The rubble of buildings wrecked by force and the wrecks of robots and planes stay after a fight (for good with War scars on: see War), and with War scars Off they grow over in the next few minutes: grass, moss and vines in the green biomes, drifting snow in the Polar Caps, sand in the Rust, ash in the Cinderlands, violet Bloom in the Bloom and the Hollow, only dust on Argos. They never get in the way: build, pave or lay track there and they're gone. From evening until dawn every Command Base shines a beam of light straight up into the sky (in the Hollow, up its breach). All of it is only for looks.
  • Mouse cursor: with a mouse, the game has its own pointer in the colours of its icon: a midnight-blue arrow edged in cream, with coral stripes on its tail. It turns coral over anything you can click, becomes a coral target over a rival when you have warbots picked (right-click to attack), shows a cream frame while you place a building or paint a road and four arrows while you drag the map. Custom cursor in the pause menu's Settings switches it off.
  • Battles: guns fire bursts of glowing tracer rounds in their colony's colour, blasts flash and roll into fireballs, wrecks burn for a while, missiles and artillery shells streak across the sky on smoke trails, and a nuclear blast leaves an eerie green glow and haze over the zone for nearly a minute. At night every shot and blast lights up the ground round it.
  • Sound: the music is a synthwave score made live as you play. It follows the game: calm while you build (three build themes take turns: Lost in a Dream, the main theme, Night Drive and Horizon, a new one every ten minutes of play), driving when you're under attack or at war (two war themes take turns, a new fight bringing the other: Overdrive, and the darker Redline, a low distorted bass grinding on under a slowly opening filter and a heavy, straight beat), low and eerie when the Murmur come close (Night Murmurs), an alarm while a nuclear missile is in the air. The Lore guide plays the campaign theme, The Long Silence. On a computer, a small card in the bottom left corner names each theme as it starts. Zoomed in, you hear what's on screen: traffic (loudest where it bunches up, with engines going by, warning beeps, toots and robots chirping round a busy factory), welding, fresh concrete, hot asphalt, truck air brakes and horns in jams, and each biome's wind. The bigger your town on screen, the busier it sounds: the hum of a city, factories clanking and roaring at work, a train's engine and the clack of its wheels (with a horn as it pulls out), planes taking off and landing, ships sounding their horns, and the sea on the shore. Away from your streets the land takes over: each biome's own sounds (leaves and grass in the wind, sand or snow hissing, insects and frogs, birds by day, the Bloom's shimmer, cracking ice, the ash plains' rumbling ground), quieter the more built-up the view. At night a cricket chorus comes in at dusk out in the wilds, with owls, a far-off night bird and the leaves stirring in the forests, peepers, frogs and the odd splash in the swamp, and a few insects and a dry wind on the savanna and in the desert; rain hushes the insects, and a fight nearby silences them for a while. Surf breaks on a sea map's shores and water laps at lakes and rivers; the ground rumbles near a volcano, louder before it erupts. The weather is heard too: rain with thunder after the lightning, gales, sandstorms, blizzards and falling embers. Near a Murmur nest or tunnel exit, now and then, a low sound in the ground, and at night over their tunnels too; in the Hollow it never stops, but for the dead silence a minute before each surge. Argos is near-silent: there's no air. Explosions are heard from where they are: up close they crack and thump, off screen they rumble in the distance. Bombs, Howitzer shells and missiles land with a deep boom. A bomb whistles as it falls, and a missile roars as it dives in, so you hear a strike coming. While enemy Vulcan Bombers are in the air coming for you, an air raid siren wails until they've gone.
  • Photo mode: for a picture of your colony, tap Photo mode in the pause menu (or press Shift+P). The game stands still (in multiplayer it runs on), the interface goes, and you frame the shot by dragging and zooming. The small bar at the bottom fades when left alone (tap the map to bring it back): slide Time of day for morning, evening or night light (only in the picture), switch on Borders or the Minimap, and Save picture downloads a PNG at your screen's full sharpness with a small Terrafall mark in the corner. Exit, Esc or Back returns to the game.
  • Observer mode: the Observer button at the foot of the main menu lets the computer play every colony while you look on. Its setup has Custom Game's options, in the same sections: two to four colonies (where the map has room; Teams with four), the Victory, the computer's level for every colony, each one's doctrine, map type, biome, size, terrain and resources (or a saved planet), the Nuclear race, planetary events, the Murmur (Off, Quiet or Active) and the challenge modifiers. Then speed it up to 8×. Tap a robot or building to look at its panel (nothing can be ordered). The Scout report under the top bar names each colony by its doctrine with its warbots, and says what they're doing (massing for a push, raiding a road, storming a Command Base); the camera follows the fighting until you move the map yourself (Follow, or F, takes it back). Nothing is saved, and your own game stays as it was.
  • Map settings and the minimap's hidden/shown state are remembered on each device.
  • Records and achievements: the Records button at the foot of the main menu shows your best on this device: the fastest win on each map type at each rival level, the longest game, the most land held and your biggest colony, with 22 achievements (winning on Hard or on every map type, beating each rival doctrine, taking a Command Base with 20 or more warbots, your first Ropeway and Freight Train, every Colony goal, your missions and more). A few, once earned, open a page of the Lore guide. The end screen says when a game beats a record or earns one. Missions, multiplayer games and games with Boosts or Unlimited resources don't count for records or wins. Challenge modifiers do (records and achievements note them), and a win with one on earns its challenge badge; a win with Fragile robots isn't a fastest win.
