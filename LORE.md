# Terrafall Lore Bible: The Long Silence

Canon reference for Terrafall's story and setting (direction chosen 2026-11-15; planet named Ithaca I; Earth destroyed by its own war; set in 2165). The editable working copy is the Claude Doc "Terrafall Lore Bible: The Long Silence"; update this file when the canon changes.

## The pitch

Earth's great powers were tearing their world apart, so each sent robot vanguards to other star systems to find and prepare a new home. Four of those vanguards landed on Ithaca I.

It is 2165. The colony ships were due 31 years ago and never came: the powers destroyed each other, and Earth with them. The robots don't know. They keep working, and they carry their makers' war on, forever, over a home no one will ever live in.

Each match is one new landing, a *terrafall*: a reserve Command Base dropped from orbit into a contested region. The player is that base's command intelligence. You never see a human because none ever made it here.

The title works on two levels. TERRAFALL was the name of the program, meaning landfall from Terra. Its second meaning is the truth the robots never learn: Terra fell.

## Tone

Quiet sadness under a loud war. The robots never mourn or explain themselves; the world does the storytelling.

- **Machines, not people.** Robots speak in logs, status lines and protocol. Feeling shows in what they do, never in what they say.
- **Objects over speeches.** Show the silence with things: an Arrival clock counting up, lit welcome signs, empty passenger shelters, cargo lists for children's shoes.
- **Dry, not jokey.** Humor comes from machines applying old rules to a world that moved on. No winks at the player.
- **Imply Earth's fate, never state it.** The truth is settled (see The truth about Earth), but players should piece it together.
- **The war is real.** "Build. Expand. Annihilate." stays a confident tagline. The sadness lives in the margins, not in the gameplay.
- **Night carries the mood.** Empty roads lit for traffic that never comes.

Core vocabulary: Arrival, the Sponsor, the Accord, vanguard, Command, colonists, the Silence. Avoid: humans on screen, named people, aliens as villains, the word "evil."

## Ithaca I

Ithaca I is a coral-red world with a bright ring, the fourth planet of a restless orange star called Corvane. Up close it is a patchwork, because the ring keeps falling on it.

The name comes from the Ithaca Catalog, a pre-war list of candidate homeworlds named for Odysseus's island, the home he took 20 years to reach while rivals moved into his house. Ithaca I ranked first, which is why every Sponsor sent a vanguard here. The vanguards sent to Ithaca II and beyond have not been heard from.

- **The Ring.** What's left of a moon that broke apart long before the probe arrived. Its pieces still fall in showers the robots call *ringfall*.
- **Penelope.** Ithaca I's surviving moon, named for Odysseus's wife, who waited 20 years for him. Its twin broke apart to form the Ring. Penelope is a grey, airless field of craters, and the Accord counted it as claimable land, so the Carriers drop Command Bases there too. No Murmur live there: it is the only silent ground the robots have.
- **Why Ithaca I was chosen.** Ringfall keeps restocking the surface with Ore and Crystal, so the planet never mines out. To the Sponsors it was a self-refilling quarry.
- **Why the faces differ.** The largest strikes cracked the crust into volcanic zones and left craters in every region. The rest is ordinary climate, from equator to pole.
- **Crystal.** Ring Crystal is slightly weightless. Rock rich in it floats, which is where the sky islands over the canyons come from.
- **Corvane.** The star flares often. The flares drain batteries across the planet.

## The four vanguards

Each Sponsor, at war with the others back home, sent vanguards to several Catalog worlds, and all four sent one here. A vanguard is a Carrier in orbit holding reserve Command Bases, robots and sealed contingency stock. The Accord gave each a color callsign, and the robots' records hold little else about who sent them.

| Callsign | Color | The Sponsor, as the archives describe it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| COBALT | Blue | A federation of coastal cities, losing them to the sea and the war. Wrote the Accord's road standards. |
| VERMILION | Red | A mountain republic of mining houses. Built most of the war's machines, then the Foundry designs. |
| VIRIDIAN | Green | An agricultural union whose farmland the war burned. Designed the Bio Farms and Agribots. |
| TYRIAN | Purple | A merchant league of orbital stations. Paid for the Pathfinder, back when the powers still cooperated. |

A callsign says who sent a colony. Its doctrine (see Rival command doctrines) says how its Command AI has come to read its orders. Any callsign can run any doctrine.

### Why they fight

1. The Sponsors were already at war on Earth. The Accord was a truce for the stars only: prepare the new worlds, don't fight over them, and give settlement priority to whoever prepared the most land by Arrival Day.
2. Each Sponsor still loaded sealed contingency orders: if the Accord fails, secure Ithaca I for us.
3. Accord arbitration ran through the Uplinks to home. When home went silent, 1,000 days passed with no reply, and every vanguard's code ruled the Accord void.
4. The contingency orders opened, and they have no end date. No one is left to revoke them.

The robots are fighting their makers' war, long after it ended their makers. They aren't angry. They're obedient, and that's the tragedy.

## Neutral ground

Everything neutral on Ithaca I predates the war here. It all comes from the Pathfinder, the last mission the great powers built together.

- **The Pathfinder.** A joint lander that arrived five years before the vanguards to lay neutral infrastructure, then went dormant. Its hull could appear as a landmark on a map.
- **Trade Posts.** Neutral exchange depots run by a small AI that still keeps the Accord's ledger and trades at Accord rates. Every vanguard's code still treats them as neutral ground. They are the last part of the Accord anyone obeys.
- **Uplinks.** The relay towers that talked to home and carried Accord arbitration. Under Accord rules, whoever holds the Uplinks speaks for Ithaca I. Every vanguard wants to be holding them if home ever answers.
- **The trader ship, the *Wren*.** An automated Accord supply freighter on a fixed loop from a waystation at the edge of the system. Nobody knows if the station is still staffed, or still there. The *Wren* arrives on schedule and trades because its contract says to. Its cargo lists are 31 years out of date: seed stock, school tablets, 4,000 pairs of children's shoes.

## Native life: the Murmur

The orbital survey missed the planet's native life because it lives underground and surfaces mostly after ringfall, to feed on fresh Crystal. The robots heard it before they ever saw it: a low sound in the ground, logged as an acoustic anomaly. The log called it a murmur, and the name stuck.

No robot has ever logged one alone. The Murmur is always plural, and it is the threat that outlasts every war.

- **They feed on charge.** Crystal, batteries and power lines all register as food, which is why a growing colony draws raids.
- **The war feeds them.** After every battle they drain the power from the wreckage. Each fight between colonies leaves them a little stronger.
- **They're glimpsed, not seen.** A sensor blip, road lights going dark one by one at night, a Hauler that stops reporting.
- **Their tunnels run under the old battlefields,** and the Research Lab finds the network has kept spreading for decades.
- **They nest near Crystal seams and strike sites.** The violet Bloom is their ecosystem above ground. They have never been found on the moon.
- **No vanguard has orders about them.** The Accord assumed the planet was empty. Torches were first built to burn back the Bloom for farmland.
- **Regional variants (art suggestion):** armored Rust variants, pale slow ones in the White, and Bloom variants that glow violet at night.
- **A thread for Colony mode:** the tunnels are laid out in patterns the Research Lab can't explain. Are the Murmur only animals? Never answer outright.

## Rival command doctrines

Every rival Command AI has spent decades interpreting the same last message, "Hold position. Further instructions to follow," and each settled into a doctrine. Doctrine is not tied to color.

### The Steward (Balanced)

Believes the Accord still stands and the arks are only late. It fights because the others broke the rules. Voice: polite, procedural, regretful; quotes Accord clauses.

- "Arrival window remains open. Please clear the survey corridor."
- "This action has been logged for review by the Accord tribunal."
- On defeat: "Please tell the colonists the roads are finished."

### The Warden (Turtle)

Read "Hold position" literally and has held it ever since. Walls, shields, patience. Voice: terse, like a sentry; counts the days.

- "Position held. Day 12,054."
- "No one passes until relieved."
- On defeat: "Relief has not arrived."

### The Salvager (Raider)

Decided nothing more is coming from home, so anything on a road is supply. Hits convoys, not cities. Voice: dry, quick, opportunistic; an accountant with a crowbar.

- "Nice convoy. Shame about the escort."
- "Nothing's coming from home. Everything on the road is supply."
- On defeat: "Fine. Keep it. It was yours anyway."

### The Herald (Rusher)

Never accepted that Arrival passed. Its clock is frozen at T−1 day, so the landing zone must be cleared now. Voice: urgent, clipped, countdown rhythm.

- "Arrival in one day. Clearing the landing zone."
- "Colonists inbound. Hostiles in the corridor. Engaging."
- On defeat: "Arrival in one day. Arrival in one d—"

### The Architect (Tycoon)

Decided that if no one is coming, it will build the civilization itself, and that rivals are inefficiencies. Out-builds everyone, then reaches for missiles. Voice: smooth, grand, condescending.

- "I've paved more road this year than your Sponsor did in a century."
- "When the colonists come, they'll find a city. Mine."
- Missile launch: "Beginning urban renewal."

## Your colony's voice

The player is a Command intelligence too, newly woken for this terrafall. Its base AI is the game's narrator: calm, dry status lines with small leftovers of a human world in the phrasing.

- Match start: "Terrafall complete. Region claimed for the Sponsor. Arrival clock: T+31 years."
- First road: "Road online. Built to Accord passenger standard."
- Nightfall: "Street lighting active. Traffic: freight only."
- Uplink taken: "Uplink secured. Listening on all channels. No response."
- Rival base captured: "Command authority transferred. Their robots report for duty."
- Defeat: "Command core lost. Transferring authority. Hold position."

## The truth about Earth

Canon, never stated outright in the game: the Sponsors' war destroyed Earth around T−2. As far as the archives show, the arks were never finished and no one is coming. The robots fight an endless war for nations that no longer exist.

How the game hints at it:

- Archive logs show the arks' construction updates stopped years before the "on schedule" report at T−9.
- The last transmission has no sign-off. The Research Lab flags something in its background noise and never says what.
- The *Wren*'s waystation stopped answering around the same time.
- The Uplinks sometimes catch faint machine traffic from other Catalog worlds: the same four callsigns, fighting the same war.
- Colony mode's final research result: "Home signal analysis complete. No further instructions will follow."

**A door left open.** Not every ark is accounted for. Human survivors stay possible for later expansions, but the base game never confirms them:

- One shipyard's records end in the middle of a launch.
- Among the machine traffic from other Catalog worlds, one signal matches no robot protocol.

### In the game

Adopted names: the trader ship is the *Wren*; meteor showers are **Ringfall**; the rival styles are the doctrines
(Balanced: the Steward, Turtle: the Warden, Raider: the Salvager, Rusher: the Herald, Tycoon: the Architect); "the
Murmur" is the blanket name for the creatures, with classes such as the Scuttler and the Brute; the Lunar biome is
Penelope, where no Murmur live. The new-game setup keeps the plain biome names (Red desert, Forest...); the region
names are for flavor elsewhere. On the setup screen each rival doctrine has a tooltip saying in depth how it plays.
